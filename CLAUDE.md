## New code: `LineupEngine/`

A Swift package (Swift 6, macOS 26, Swift Testing, Foundation only). Built so far: the domain model, the FMRTE clipboard importer, the squad book, the FM27 role catalogue and tactics, the rating core, fitness and risk rules, an exact assignment solver, lineup selection with a reason for every pick and omission, the planner (looking ahead across fixtures), and the advisors (First and Second XI, Depth, Rest, Training, Player Removal, Mentoring, a suggested importance for each fixture, a game plan for a simulated match, and Scouting). The app is a second package, `LineupApp/`, described below. The engine has no UI and reads no files itself; it is handed data.

Test it with:

    cd LineupEngine && swift test

Run one suite or test with `swift test --filter <name>`. Test output must be clean: no build warnings.

Try it on the captured squad (prints the lineup with reasons; add `--profile both` to compare rating profiles):

    cd LineupEngine && swift run -q lineup-cli select \
      --grids Tests/LineupEngineTests/Fixtures/captures/first-team.grids.json \
      --tactic Tests/LineupEngineTests/Fixtures/tactics/sample-4231.json \
      --first-team Tests/LineupEngineTests/Fixtures/captures/first-team.tsv \
      --u21 Tests/LineupEngineTests/Fixtures/captures/u21.tsv --own-club "Oxford City"

How the engine works, where it is not obvious:

- **Import.** FMRTE copies rows with no header. Column meaning comes from FMRTE's own `config.json` (`ColumnsSettings`), and the importer picks the grid whose column types fit the paste. Squads are imported one at a time (first team, U21, U18). Wages are read in the period FMRTE's settings file names (its top-level `Wages`: Weekly, Monthly or Yearly; none is weekly) and the currency symbol on the pasted amounts, which must be the same on every row. They are kept per week inside the engine (`Player.wagePerWeek`, not always whole), and shown as FMRTE shows them through the squad book's `moneyStyle` (`MoneyStyle`, `Display.wage`); squads held in different currencies raise a squad warning, since there is no exchange rate.
- **Identity.** A player's key is FMRTE's `Id` column when the pasted grid has it (`fmrte:<id>`); without it, players are identified by name, with a warning. `SquadBook` keeps keys stable across imports and detects moves between squads.
- **Rating profiles.** Ratings come from a `RatingProfile`, which `RatingEngine` and `LineupSelector` take explicitly. `RatingProfile.standard()` is "FM27 engine tests", the owner's chosen default: outfield weights and attribute curves from FM27 match-engine tests (`Resources/rating_profile_fm27.json`), with goalkeeper weights and the way weights differ between positions taken from the FM26 tables (`Resources/rating_tables.json`). "Carried over from FM26" is the old Python's weights, kept for comparison. Design section 8.4 has the formulas.
- **The port check.** The `.fm26Reference` configuration reproduces the old Python (role thresholds, current-ability factor, rounding) and is tested against 594 cases exported from it. Nothing else uses it.
- **Selection.** Rating times condition, sharpness and jadedness factors, solved exactly as an assignment problem, with manual overrides and risk rules that depend on match importance. The owner's choice of a player for a slot beats every rule that would leave him out except being out on loan, and the pick says which rule it overrules. That includes an injury: FMRTE says that a player is injured, not whether he can play through it, and the game passes some injured players fit to play (the owner's report, 18 November 2026). Nothing but the owner's choice picks an injured player, and the choice holds for that fixture only. FMRTE shows that a player is banned, not which matches the ban is for, so a fixture says whether bans count in it (`Fixture.bansApply`); a banned player misses the earliest planned fixture where they do, and no other.
- **Planning.** `Planner` picks the lineups for the next fixtures in date order. Each player's value is adjusted, with a reason, by the importance rules (consecutive starts, sharpness, development, marked to sell or loan out, loans, retraining, stability) and by a cost for starting him when a bigger fixture follows soon. A player the owner has marked to sell or to loan out is favoured slightly (`shopWindowFactor`, ×1.03) in low and sharpness fixtures only; the size is the tool's own estimate, and whether playing a player brings offers is untested. A player of 23 or under with potential at least 1.15 times his ability, and a reach that is not markedly lower or a place as first choice at a slot he can play (see Advisors), is favoured in low and sharpness fixtures at every slot he can play, by age: ×1.10 at 21 to 23, ×1.07 at 18 to 20, ×1.02 at 17 or under (×1.04 at 17 or under with Professionalism 14 or under). A successor is favoured more, in place of that favour, at the slot where he is close to passing the first choice (a player of 18 to 23 who is close or on course; 17 or under only when close): ×1.15 in low and sharpness fixtures and ×1.05 in medium ones, never in high ones; in a medium fixture only the two closest successors who can be picked are favoured. A first choice at any slot is nobody's successor, a player on loan here gets no favour (the owner's choice: his own players are developed), and neither does a player Player Removal advises to release, sell or terminate, who also takes no place among the two successors at medium; the planner is handed that set (`PlanRequest.advisedToLeave`), which the app and the command line's `plan` work out with Player Removal on the advice's inputs, not at all with Development at 0, and as nobody when the advice fails. A successor who is also retraining at the slot gets whichever is larger: the successor's favour alone, or the floor and the retraining favour together as a non-successor would have them (the floor and retraining on a tie); never all three, and one who would carry the successor's favour at no slot of a medium fixture takes no place among the two. Both favours fall to nothing once he has started four matches in the last thirty days, planned starts included, since the game reads only so much match time in a month (carried over from FM26). The Development setting, 0 to 1 and 1 by default, beside Stability and Saving players, scales each favour as 1 + (factor − 1) × the setting; at 0 no lineup changes, and no outlook is worked out, so a young player missing Professionalism, Ambition or Determination does not stop the plan. The sizes are derived from the research's growth per match by age; the outlooks are worked out once for each tactic with the same code Player Removal uses. Condition, sharpness and jadedness are projected from one fixture to the next; starters are assumed to play 90 minutes and nobody else plays.
- **Advisors.** They advise; the planner decides who starts. Each is a function of the data it is handed (`Advisors/`). `Elevens` gives the best and second-best elevens at full fitness, injured players kept in and marked; Depth, Training and Player Removal build on those tiers. Depth calls a slot thin when fewer than two players count as cover there: solid (familiarity 15 or over in both phases), rated at least 80% of the slot's first choice there (`depthCoverShare`, carried over from FM26, added on 18 November 2026 after a player at 74% kept a slot of the owner's from being thin), not injured and not first choice elsewhere; a one-day dip in condition does not stop a player counting. A solid player under the 80% is still a stand-in (`DepthOption.isStandIn`, `SlotDepth.standIns`): Player Removal protects real cover at a slot whose cover is at most the line, and also a stand-in too weak to count where the stand-ins are at most the line ("Too few players can step in at left back without him"), so the only other body at a slot is kept until the squad has someone better. Training recommends position retraining for thin slots, and for Training cover that Player Removal advises moving on does not count: a slot is thin when fewer than two of its cover are staying, and the need names who is going (`RetrainingNeed.going`), since Depth still counts him. It also lists the accepted retrainings no thin slot needs (`TrainingReport.retrainingsNotNeeded`). `RetrainingTracker` calls a retraining behind when, over at least six weeks, it has gained something but at under half (`retrainingBehindShare`) the pace that reaches solid in the weeks `Training.estimatedWeeks` gives for the player from where he started; with no gain it is stalled, as before. The planner favours a retrainee at his new position only from familiarity 10, so the plan never plays him there before that (a limit left: the owner's two retrainings had no start in their new positions in thirty confirmed matches); Rest flags players over their own jadedness limit, under the condition floor or on a run of starts; Player Removal scores every player of the club's own with a points reason for each rule, and reviews loans to the club; it reads a youth contract (FMRTE pastes `Youth`) and costs it like a full-time one, and a player on one can only be kept or released, at any age, never sold, terminated, loaned or marked (one engine function, `Removal.isOnYouthContract`, reads it; with 0 months left it is read as no contract). Player Removal costs a release as the wages for the months left plus half a month, because FMRTE's months left is rounded down. A player of 23 or under is judged on his reach, which is this tool's estimate and never a fact: his ability after each coming season is the growth for his age (18 points a season to 18, 14 to 20 and 12 to 26, from FM24 and FM26 tests) scaled for Professionalism, Ambition and Determination, never above his potential, and added up to age 26. The reach is compared, at each slot he can play, with the first and second choice there (a rating is taken to grow with ability from an offset, `ratingAbilityOffset`, fitted on the captured squads and loosely pinned, not in proportion). One line replaces the old elevens, weakest-30%, ability and prospect lines: could pass the first choice (−40), could make the second eleven (−20), could become cover (+15) or markedly lower (+70); a place in either eleven competes with that line, and a backup takes it. The bar between cover and markedly lower is 80% of the first choice's rating, 70% at 16 or under; below it he is markedly lower whoever the second choice is. His Professionalism and Ambition add no line, since his reach already counts them, and a short contract on a player worth developing (one who is first choice at a slot he can play, whatever his level at his other slots, could pass the first choice or make the second eleven, or has no level) says "renew it" at no points; for one not worth developing it keeps its words and points. The advice for him is one of four, with the engine's own sentences and each row showing its working (which says "now, fewer later" only when a later season's growth for his age is lower, at 20 or under, and not from 21, where it is the same every season): keep and develop (17 or under; or, when he is not getting his matches and is not needed as cover, on a youth contract or with twelve months or fewer left, or months unknown), keep and play (he is first choice, or has started a third of the confirmed matches of the last 60 days, or, with fewer than six confirmed, is a solid second choice), loan for matches (18 to 23, not on a youth contract, with more than twelve months left, worth developing, not getting his matches and not needed as cover) or watch (a player worth developing who is not getting his matches and is needed as cover, or one not worth developing who is not kept to play); one not worth developing (cover at best or markedly lower, and first choice at no slot he can play) is never developed or loaned, and is kept to play only when he is in an eleven and playing. A loan for matches is not a removal and is not counted among those advised to leave. These replaced the old prospect and false-prospect rules, except that a false prospect on a full-time contract is sold, not terminated. A player of 24 and over is scored as before; `ImportanceAdvisor` suggests an importance from the competition, the opponent and the schedule, and the owner's choice always wins (a league match starts from a base set by `LeagueSituation`, which the owner chooses: title race or relegation fight 100, in contention 80 (the default and the value every league match had before), mid-table 60, nothing to play for 20; the bases are carried over from FM26's table, which that app never called, and the owner says when they apply; cups and friendlies are unchanged; `--league-situation` sets it on the command line). `OpponentAdvisor.read` reads an opponent's strength from his pasted squad: their club is the most common club on the rows when they carry one (FMRTE's team grid has none, so otherwise the opponent as typed on the fixture); a player loaned to another club is left out; each side's best eleven are its eleven fit players with the highest current ability, whatever their positions, and their average over the owner's (every squad he holds) is compared by the lines `opponentStrengthLines`: 5% above or below is stronger or weaker and 15% much stronger or much weaker (the tool's own estimate, with no test behind it). It refuses a paste whose players are mostly the owner's own (or whose rows carry his club), a side with fewer than eleven fit players, and rows with no club when the opponent is blank, each in a sentence. The reading (club, day, the two averages, the strength, the clubs of those left out) is kept on the fixture; the opponent's players are kept nowhere and enter no advisor. `GamePlanAdvisor.advise` says which game plans to use and to avoid when a match is simulated, by the opponent's strength on the fixture and nothing else, from one entry in `EngineParameters` (`gamePlanRules`, carried over from FM26: the table of `docs/Research/fm26-match-simulation-game-plans.md`). Against a much stronger side: Play for the draw or Play for the win, never Settle for a draw, Strangle the game or Smash and grab; that is the one row that rests on a test of the plans (FM26, ten results a plan), whose points are kept with the rule and quoted. Against a stronger side: Play for the win or Play for the draw. Against a similar or weaker side: Play for the win or No game plan. With no strength set: Play for the win. Those rows are the research's reasoning and carry "this tool's estimate"; the sentences say which is which, and that No game plan was not in the test. Where two plans are named nothing separates them. A rule with no plan to use, or a plan both to use and to avoid, is a parameter problem; Mentoring (`Mentoring.report`) has its own entry point and is worked out per squad. It is handed the squad, the owner's entries (each player's hierarchy level and social group, and when they were entered), the groups the owner has recorded and the personality readings kept at each import, and it suggests groups of three from arrows. An arrow says how strongly one player of a group influences another (None, Light, Average or Significant, the game's words). The owner reads arrows off the game's screen and the engine keeps them for each pair of players; where none has been read it guesses, from the source's hierarchy level and the target's age (23 or under), and says on every line that it is a guess ("expected, not read") and, for one that was read, the day it was read. A group is any three players with no harmful arrow in it, where an arrow is harmful when it is above None and its source is worth less than its target on a counted attribute. A candidate's gain in a group is that of the one arrow into him with the greatest gain (a Light arrow from a much better player can beat a Significant one), never the sum. A player the owner's readings show takes nothing (None from at least three different sources at Influential or above, and nothing above None) is listed as not placed, and can still make up a group as its third member. Of two groups worth the same, the one with fewer members who will go (on loan here or advised to leave) is suggested first, before the older ties (fewer members above Others, more arrows read, the keys), and any member who will go carries the caution that the group ends when he goes (section 13b of the design, added on 15 November 2026 after a group the owner had ended was suggested again unchanged). What was seen of a group the owner ended counts for more than what its arrows promise (section 13c, added on 16 November 2026 after a group ended for doing harm was suggested again): the same three are not suggested again, in a suggestion or a regrouping, when the group was doing harm or had stopped working on the day it ended, judged on the readings up to that day; a candidate whose every helpful group is such a group is not placed, and says so; and a suggested group that puts a member who fell with another member of the group he fell in carries a caution saying what fell and that the cause is not known. Standing above a player by age, a lead for the group and the halving for a shared social group no longer exist. Values are shown as "Professionalism 8, towards 17". It predicts nothing: no gain or score is shown, only the strength and the two values. It also reviews recorded groups (what each member's values did, a warning when a group pulls someone down, a warning when all six of its arrows were read as None, and a warning when it has stopped working after thirteen weeks). It says to break a recorded group up when no member of the club's own who is staying has anything to gain from it by its arrows: as what the tool expects while any of the six is a guess, and not at all while the squad has no level entered to guess from. And it gives at most one regrouping: one recorded group, or two together, whose present members, grouped with the free players in no suggested group by the rule that forms the suggestions, would count for more and for at least 1.5 times as much (`mentoringRegroupFactor`, the tool's own choice; a recorded group with a harmful arrow or with no gain counts for nothing, and one whose gain is not known is never broken up). The suggested groups are left as they are, and the count predicts nothing. It lists players it could not place, with a reason (takes nothing, nobody to influence him, nothing to gain because nobody who influences him is better anywhere it counts, too few players free, no third player free who could join him and a player who could help him without pulling anyone down, his possible influencers already taken, or every helpful group would pull someone down, which is also said when an influencer better in one value is worse by more in another). Every piece of advice carries the weakest evidence behind it, and most of the numbers are carried over from FM26 or the tool's own estimates.
- **Scouting** (`Advisors/Scouting.swift` for who is going, the squad that stays and the needs; `Advisors/ScoutingCandidates.swift` for who is already held, a candidate's judgement and the list; `Reporting/AdvisorText+Scouting.swift` for every word). It is for the squad the owner will have, so it is judged without the players who are going. A player is going when he is on loan at the club, marked to sell, marked to loan out, or advised to leave by Player Removal (`advisedToLeave`; a loan for matches is not among them), and each is counted once, under the first of those reasons in that order. `Scouting.going` works the set out; the command line takes no marks, so it has only the first and last. The elevens and Depth are solved again for the chosen tactic from the players who stay (with nobody going they are the Elevens screen's own), and everything below is judged on them: "first choice" and "second choice" mean those among the players who stay. The need for a slot is of two kinds, with no ranking between them, since nothing measures a weak first choice against a missing stand-in. **A better player**, the first of these that holds: nobody (no first choice, or he is out of position there, with familiarity under 10 in either phase); weak (his rating is 5% or more under the average rating of the outfield first choices, those out of position left out of the average; the weak line, `scoutingWeakShare`, is 0.95 and labelled derived: this tool's estimate with no test behind it); ageing (he is 30 or over, `firstChoiceAgeNamedFrom`, and no player of the club's own of 23 or under, staying or out on loan and not first choice at another slot, is close or on course to pass him at the slot by the bands Player Removal and the planner use; the line says decline is not measured, so it is a prompt to look). The goalkeeper is rated on his own scale, so he is compared by current ability, both his and the outfield first choices' taken through `ratingAbilityOffset` (the way a rating is taken to grow with ability), against the same line, and the line says ability was compared. When today's first choice at the slot is going, the line names him and why, and judges the best who stays ("Sam Reed, the best who stays, rates …"). A weak first choice of 30 or over is named with his age ("Alfie Potter, 37, rates 5.9% under …"), since the weak rule comes before the ageing one and would otherwise hide it. A nobody or weak need is eased when a player of the club's own of 23 or under, staying or out on loan, has a reach rating at the slot that clears the weak line (for the goalkeeper, his reach as ability through the offset), the highest reach naming him; one out on loan is rated at the slot directly, as a pasted player is, against the staying rivals, and the line says where he is ("Max Treml, out on loan at Wallingford, is 18; …"). The ageing rule's successors are the same players: of 23 or under (`developingUpToAge`), staying or out on loan, close or on course to pass him (found on the captured squad: the goalkeeper's line said nobody was on course while a goalkeeper out on loan was). A player who is first choice at another slot eases nothing and is nobody's successor, since he cannot fill both (found on the captured squad: the need at left defensive midfield was shown as eased by the first choice at right defensive midfield). An eased need stays on the list, after the others of its kind, and its line says "his reach would bring him level (this tool's estimate)"; an ageing need is never eased. One helper holds each rule: the weak line (`Scouting.isWeak`, `clearsWeakLine`), the goalkeeper's slot (`isGoalkeeperSlot`, on the in-possession position; the text is handed what was measured and asks nothing about positions) and Depth's cover test (`Depth.isCover`). **More depth**: Depth's own count among those who stay, fewer than two counting as cover (solid, familiarity 15 or over in both phases, rated at least 80% of the first choice, not injured, not first choice elsewhere), with Depth's numbers and sentences, so that the Elevens and Training screens agree. The lines give the count and who counts; the next best against the slot's first choice as a percentage ("The next best, Zac McEachran, rates 6.4% under Fernando Forestieri.", "… rates 2.0% above …" when he rates higher, "The next best is …" with no first choice, "Nobody else can step in." with none); who would count but for an injury, since an injured player is not cover ("Not counting Fernando Forestieri, who is injured."), and, when counting them would lift the slot to the line, "The need lasts only while he is out."; cover who counted today and is going ("Not counting Scott Pollock, who is going"); and players out on loan who are solid there and within that share and would count as cover on their return. Better players are listed nobody first, then weak by the size of the gap, then ageing oldest first, ties by the tactic's slot order; more depth in Depth's order (least cover, then the larger drop to the next best), the needs that last only while players are injured after the others; the slots with neither are listed last as fine. The evidence on a need is the weakest among the entries it read. **A pasted player** (`Scouting.candidates`) is one the owner does not hold. He is held when his key and a held player's are both FMRTE ids and the same, when his club or the club he is on loan at is the owner's, or when a held player has the same name, age and potential ability (the search grid has no id column, so the last rule is how the owner's own players are known); names alone are not matched, so a namesake at another club stays, and the rules are applied again each time the players are judged, so one the owner has since signed and imported drops out. He is judged at each slot of the tactic where his familiarity is 10 or over in both phases, by the full-fitness rating the Elevens and Depth use; condition, sharpness and jadedness play no part and an injury is only marked. **Today**, against the first and second choice who stay: would start (at least the first choice's rating, or no first choice there), would make the second eleven (at least the second choice's, or there is none in position), would count as cover (at least 80% of the first choice's, `depthCoverShare`) or below cover; the line names who he was compared with and the gap ("rates 6.0% above Ellis Brown, who fills in at left back out of position" when the first choice is out of position there), says when he is not yet solid there (with the two familiarities), and says when he would start at a weak or nobody slot and still be under the weak line himself (at the goalkeeper's slot with the two abilities). **Later**, only for a player of 23 or under (`developingUpToAge`): his reach is `Reach.estimate`, as for Player Removal's young players, and his level (could pass the first choice, could make the second eleven, could become cover, markedly lower), band and working come from the same code, made to take a player who is not in the squad. Every sentence about his reach names its slot ("Not expected to reach the level of cover at left back"); "at any position", Player Removal's words for a squad player, is never said of a candidate, whose other slots may differ. At a slot with no first choice there is no level, and the line gives his reach and whether it clears the weak line ("His reach would rate about 112 at left back, which clears the line for a weak slot", by ability at the goalkeeper's slot). For one who would only be cover the line says the estimate assumes regular matches. A player of 24 or over is "judged as he is today", and from 30 the line says decline is not measured. **Cautions** are Player Removal's own lines (`removalRules`, carried over from FM26), with the value: Consistency, Important Matches, Injury Proneness, Controversy, Temperament and Professionalism on the wrong side of theirs; they carry no points and change no level. He **answers a better-player need** today when he would start at the slot, and later when he is 23 or under and could pass the first choice there; he **answers a depth need** when Depth would count him as cover there (solid, and at least 80% of the first choice's rating, or solid alone with no first choice), whatever his level, so a player far below the first choice who would make the second eleven only because nobody else is there does not. His **headline slot** is the first of: a slot where he answers a better-player need today (in the needs' order); one where he answers a depth need (in theirs); one where he answers a better-player need later; a slot where he would start (the greatest margin over the first choice, then the tactic's order); a slot where, at 23 or under, his reach could pass the first choice (the greatest reach margin, then the tactic's order); otherwise where his level today is best, then his margin is greatest, then the earlier slot, where a place in the second eleven he would hold only because nobody in position is there, rating under 80% of the first choice, ranks below would count as cover. His **other slots** are shown with the same lines for today as the headline slot and, at 23 or under, one line for later ("Later: could pass Deago Nelson at right back."; `AdvisorText.otherSlotLines`). The **list** has five parts, each player once, under his headline slot: answers a need today (the better-player slots in their order, then the depth slots in theirs), could answer a need later (grouped the same way), would start where there is no need, could pass a first choice where there is no need, and the rest (a player who can play no slot is here, last, and says so). The owner picks the order (`CandidateOrder`, Today or Later; it never moves a player between parts): answers today by rating today, or reach rating in Later; could answer later by reach rating in both; would start elsewhere by his margin over the first choice, or his reach margin (reach rating over what the first choice is compared at, minus 1) in Later; could pass elsewhere by reach margin in both; the rest by margin, or reach margin in Later. A player with no reach is placed by today's figure in Later, and the player's key breaks every tie. Each need and each candidate carries the weakest evidence behind it (`basis`), and the candidates' report the weakest among them (`CandidatesReport.basis`); the app shows an evidence note on both tabs. Nothing is predicted and no player or squad is given one score: today and later are two verdicts, and the needs and the reach are this tool's estimates, each with its working.

Tools:

- `tools/capture_fmrte_paste.sh <name>` saves the clipboard and FMRTE's player-grid column layouts as a test fixture. How each capture was taken: `LineupEngine/Tests/LineupEngineTests/Fixtures/captures/README.md`.
- `tools/export_rating_reference.py` regenerates `rating_tables.json` and the Python reference cases from the old code. Both outputs are tracked; do not re-run it without a reason.

Rules for the engine:

- No silent defaults. A missing column, unknown role, or blank or out-of-range value is an error that names the player and the column.
- Every tunable number for a rating or selection rule lives in `EngineParameters` or a `RatingProfile`, with an evidence label (FM27 measured, FM27 official, FM27 observation, carried over from FM26, derived) and a source. A `Parameter` keeps its value off the stack and is one pointer wide: `EngineParameters` goes down every call by value, and the engine's tests run on small stacks, so keep it small (a test holds it under 1 KB).
- Every rule gets a test where it changes an outcome and a test where it does not.
- The same inputs always give the same lineup: sums run in a fixed order.
- Errors and warnings become plain-words sentences in `Reporting/ErrorDescriptions.swift`; every `switch` over them is exhaustive, with no `default`.

## New code: `LineupApp/`

The Mac app: a second Swift package beside the engine, depending on it by path (Swift 6, macOS 26, SwiftUI and Observation, Swift Testing, no third-party code). Three targets:

- `LineupAppCore`: the app's state and every action, with no views. `AppModel` (observable, on the main actor) holds the squads, tactics, fixtures, confirmed lineups and settings, saves each change through `Store` as versioned JSON, and works out the plan by calling the engine's `Planner`, with Player Removal's players advised to leave worked out for each plan. It works out the advice (the engine's `Advisors`, and each fixture's suggested importance) when it is first read after a change, and keeps it until the next. Everything a screen shows that is built from data (labels, rows, which fixture is chosen, status words) is worked out here, with tests.
- `LineupAppUI`: the SwiftUI views and `Theme`. Views hold layout only.
- `LineupApp`: the few lines that start the app.

Besides Squad, Tactics, Fixtures, Match Selection and Settings, the app has the advisor screens: Elevens (First XI, Second XI and Depth, for any saved tactic; in Depth a solid player rated under 80% of the first choice has a line saying he is not counted as cover), Rest, Training (a Retraining tab: accept or turn down a recommended retraining, and follow its progress at each import, where a retraining that has stalled or is well behind its estimate, and one no thin slot needs, says so as a caution in the engine's sentence; and a Mentoring tab, below), Player Removal (with the loans to the club, and a Young players part showing each player of 23 or under with his reach and its working, his standing at his slot and his advice, stamped in the tone of his level's stamp in the table above, except that "Loan for matches" is drawn in ink where that tone would be muted; an opened row marks the player to sell or to loan out, except for a player on a youth contract, whose control is replaced by the engine's sentence saying he can only be kept or released, and a mark is dropped at the next import if he is on a youth contract, once every squad held has been imported without him, once he is on loan here, or, for a loan mark, once he is out on loan) and Scouting (stands after Player Removal, with the same tactic menu as Elevens, the same gate and the same line naming the day the advice rests on, and two tabs: Needs and Candidates). Needs names who is counted as going, then the "Better players" and "More depth" lists, each slot on its token ("D L") with the engine's lines and the evidence note, an eased need drawn muted, then the slots that are fine. Candidates has "Paste players", "Clear" and an "Order by" control (Today or Later), the line "6 players read." and the engine's line naming those left out as already the owner's, then the five parts with a group under each slot and need; each player's row has his age, positions, club ("Free agent" for one out of contract) and, where the paste carried them, "On loan at", asking price and wage in the paste's own currency and period, an injury stamp, his lines for today and later, a labelled line of cautions, and, when the row is opened, his other slots, each slot's lines together; the last part, "The rest", starts closed behind a line saying how many are in it (Core says which group is closed and behind what line); the evidence note is under the list, and the rows are in a lazy stack so a long paste lays out only what is on screen. "Paste players" reads the clipboard through the squad importer with FMRTE's settings file, as the opponent paste does; it refuses, in the engine's sentence and with what was held kept, a clipboard or settings file that cannot be read, rows the importer refuses, and a paste in which every player is already the owner's, and the importer's note that players are identified by name is not shown. "Clear" is offered while players are held or a refusal is shown, and removes both. The pasted players are held in the model's memory only: never saved through `Store`, gone when the app quits or at "Clear", replaced by the next paste, judged again when read after an import, a tactic change or a mark, and entering no other advice. A paste does not plan, work the other advice out again, or touch the squad book, marks, mentoring file or any file (`AppModel+Scouting.swift`, `ScoutingPresentation.swift`; the views are `ScoutingScreen.swift`, `ScoutingNeedsTab.swift` and `ScoutingCandidatesTab.swift`; the row type is `ScoutingCandidateRow`, since Core already had a `CandidateRow` for Match Selection's picker). The date note at the top of every screen has "Next match" beside "Day before" and "Day after": it moves today in the game to the day of the next match (`AppModel.nextMatch`: the earliest fixture from today on with no lineup confirmed), and is disabled when that match is today or there is none. Fixtures shows each fixture's suggested importance beside the one chosen; its table opens scrolled so the next match is in the middle, with the matches already played above it, and scrolls only as far as needed when another becomes the next match. A "League situation" menu in its heading row (shown when there are fixtures) sets where the club stands, kept in the settings (absent in older files, read as in contention); it works the suggestions out again and does not plan. Under each suggestion line the table offers "Use it", which sets that fixture's importance to the suggested one and plans again; it is ignored within half a second of the last one (`AppModel.suggestionActionInterval`) and is not offered for the fixture being edited. In the fixture editor, "Paste their squad" beside "Their strength" reads the clipboard through the squad importer, sets the draft's strength and shows the engine's sentence with the two averages, or the refusal; nothing is saved until the fixture is. It keeps only the reading on the fixture, and does not touch the squad book, marks, mentoring file or plan. A strength picked by hand afterwards stands. Match Selection's candidate picker lists those who can play, then those a rule leaves out with the reason in red (injured, banned, below the condition floor, jaded, left out by the owner); any of them can be chosen, since the engine uses the choice over the rule, and only a player who cannot be rated cannot. Match Selection's notes across the top open scrolled to the match chosen (the next to play when the screen opens), so the matches already confirmed are off to the left, and scroll only as far as needed when another is chosen. When a player who could play is banned, the fixture his ban is served in says so under "Left out", in the engine's sentence, with a button to say the bans are not for that match; they are then picked there and left out of the next fixture that takes bans, and that fixture offers "Apply bans to this match". What the owner said is kept on the fixture (absent in older files); a friendly takes no bans unless he says so. For a fixture with a planned eleven it says which game plans to use if the match is simulated: one line at the top of the column beside the pitch and a "Game plan" part under "Left out", in the engine's sentences with its evidence note; a confirmed or missed fixture shows neither. Settings has the Development slider beside Stability and Saving players. When a plan or the advice cannot be worked out, the notice leads to the screen that can put it right (a missing player value, or two players with one identity, to Squad, a fixture problem to Fixtures, bad settings to Settings) and offers no button where no screen can.

The Mentoring tab shows, for one squad at a time, the hierarchy, suggested groups, the owner's recorded groups, a regrouping when the engine has one ("Regrouping to try": its sentences and the groups it would make, with nothing to press, since the groups to break up are ended on their own cards and their players are then suggested) and the players who could not be placed. Each group is shown as its arrows, one line each. A recorded group's card says across its top what is wrong with it, and that a regrouping would break it up. FMRTE exports neither the hierarchy, the groups nor the arrows, so the owner enters the hierarchy by hand (a level for each player; a social group is still entered and kept but changes nothing), chooses a group's three members in an editor, and reads the arrows off the game in the arrows sheet. The sheet opens three ways: "Record arrows" beside "Add a group", "Record arrows" on a suggested group that rests on guesses, and "Arrows" on a recorded group; it also opens on its own after a new group is saved. Its "Set the rest to None" button sets every arrow not yet read to None, since the game shows only those above None. A reading is kept for each pair in the mentoring file, with its day, and fills the same pair in any later group; a read arrow is shown bold and a guess muted. A personality reading of every player is kept at each import, so what a group changed can be shown. The mentoring advice is worked out apart from the other advice, so a failure in one does not hide the other, and entering or recording something does not plan again. Tests show that saved state from before this plan, and from before the arrows, still opens with no notice; opening the real app on such a state has not been checked by hand.

The owner's own readings are a test fixture in `LineupEngine/Tests/LineupEngineTests/Fixtures/mentoring/`: the arrows of 24 groups of the first team as the game showed them on 14 November 2026, and the first team's ages, levels and personality values that day. The guess is checked against them (it agrees exactly with 104 of 124 pairs), and so are the suggestions made from them. `README.md` there says how they were taken.

Rules for the app:

- The engine decides; the app shows. A view never computes a rating, a reason or a rule. Sentences for errors, warnings and reasons are the engine's (`ErrorDescriptions.describe`, and the reasons on picks and omissions), shown as written. Where the engine has no sentence for something, add one to the engine.
- Every word about advice (level names, actions, verdicts, evidence notes and sentences) comes from the engine (`AdvisorText`, `PositionText`, and the reasons on the reports), shown as written. Core holds only plain labels and headings.
- The Training screen ignores a training action within half a second of the end of the last one (`AppModel.trainingActionInterval`): accepting or turning down a card moves the next card's buttons under the pointer, so a double-click would otherwise act on the next player too.
- Logic for a screen lives in `LineupAppCore` with tests. If a view needs an `if` about data, that is a sign it belongs in Core.
- The look is the Tactics board in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-13-fm27-app-visual-direction.md`. Its two typefaces, Bricolage Grotesque and Figtree, ship with their licences in `LineupAppUI`'s resources and are registered when the app starts. Every colour, typeface and measure comes from `Theme`; no view holds a literal colour, font name or measure.
- Nothing the owner reads is an id or a code: slots are "D L" on a token and "left back" in a sentence; fixtures are named by opponent and date.
- A file that cannot be read gives a notice and a fresh start for that file, and the unreadable file is kept aside. Nothing in a test, a picture run or a sample-data run touches `~/Library/Application Support` or the real clipboard.
- Unsaved changes to a tactic are kept in the model until saved or reverted. Only the earliest planned fixture can be confirmed. A confirmed match keeps its load on its starters in later fixtures until their squad is imported again.

## What the old Python runs, and what it never connected

Runs (per a traced reading of the code):

- Rating core (`rating_calculator.py`): per-player, per-slot rating from position attribute weights, the role's key and secondary attributes, a familiarity factor per phase, and an in-possession/out-of-possession blend.
- Match selection (`ui/api/api_match_selector.py` over `fm_match_ready_selector.py`): that rating times condition, sharpness and jadedness factors, solved as an assignment problem, with manual overrides, per-match rejections, a lineup-stability term, consecutive-start counts from confirmed lineups, and auto-rest rules.
- Advisors: First and Second XI (`api_rotation_selector.py`), Rest (`api_rest_advisor.py`), Training (`api_training_advisor.py` over `fm_training_advisor.py`), Player Removal (`api_player_removal.py`).

Written but never connected (design references, not behaviour to match):

- `state_simulation.py`: forward simulation of fitness.
- `shadow_pricing.py`: shadow pricing beyond a display value.
- `explainability.py`: pick explanations.
- `match_importance.py`: automatic match importance.

The old 155 tests exercise the unconnected modules and parameter helpers. Nothing tests the path that produces lineups.
