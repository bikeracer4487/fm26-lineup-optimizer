# What clubs accept for a sale or a loan in FM27: buyers' budgets, not values, set prices

Researched on 22 November 2026, with FM27 at 27.1.0.

Nobody has published a test, a datamine or a Sports Interactive statement, for FM27 or any earlier version, that says what fee or what loan terms an AI club accepts from a human manager; FM27's only words on the subject are one patch line, "Tweaks to AI transfer balancing", in 27.1.0. The answer is therefore a procedure built from labelled guesses, not a formula. **To sell**: first confirm a buyer exists (active interest, or a TransferRoom requirement the player fits), because without one the reported outcome at tiers 5 to 6 is no bid or a bid at 5 to 10% of displayed value; then ask for a total near the player's stored value, with the cash part at or under the buyer's remaining transfer budget (a median of about £10K in the National League, £0 in National League North and South, £35K in League Two and £100K in League One on day one of FM27), and the rest as instalments, an appearance add-on and a 20% sell-on of the next fee; do not transfer-list, do not accept a first bid, and counter once at about 20% more. **To loan out**: propose the terms yourself, since AI clubs open at 0% wages and squad player; ask the wage share given by the smallest of 100%, (value ÷ 192) ÷ weekly wage, and the borrower's free wage room ÷ weekly wage, rounded down to the game's step; ask Regular Starter with a recall clause for a development loan; and expect no monthly fee at this level. That loan rule is the Substack rule, and it is **value ÷ 48 a month, not value ÷ 12** as `docs/Research/fm27-selling-and-loans.md` words it. The hidden values that bear most on both deals are the player's stored transfer value, which the owner's FMRTE paste does not carry today, and the buying club's Transfer Budget Remaining, Wage Budget less Wage Used, and "Max Wage (aprox.)", all on FMRTE 27's club Finances page. Every number here is carried over from an earlier version, a tool author's heuristic or this report's own guess; the Sports Interactive forums, Reddit and YouTube could not be read; and about a dozen reload tests in the owner's own save would produce the first measured sample that exists in public for any version.

## No test in any version says what an AI club accepts

This report keeps five kinds of claim apart. **Tested** means someone compared numbers and published the comparison. **Official** means Sports Interactive said it, in a feature page, a manual or patch notes (where a patch line reached these notes through a news site's copy, it is marked secondary). **Tool-documented** means the documentation or source code of FMRTE 27, FMSuperScout or FMLibreEditor says a field exists or is shown; a tool's own multipliers are its author's heuristics and are called that. **Anecdotal** means a forum post or guide with no data, and **speculation** means an inference made in the research or in this report. Anything from before FM27 is marked "carried over from" its version. There is **no finding labelled "FM27, tested" on fees, bids or loan terms**, because none was found.

What could not be read matters as much as what was. **Sports Interactive's forums, bug tracker and online manual returned HTTP 403 to every fetch**, and the Wayback Machine could not be fetched either, so every SI forum or bug-tracker item below was seen only as a search engine's summary of the page and is marked "snippet only"; no SI staff answer on valuation was read in full, and no list of open FM27 bugs could be made. **Reddit could not be read at all** (old.reddit.com was blocked and Reddit appeared in no search result), which includes the two Reddit comments the Substack loan rule cites as its origin. **YouTube could not be read**, so a video test with no write-up cannot be ruled out. Sortitoutsi and footballmanagerblog.org also returned 403, two fm-arena comment pages returned 404, and two later Substack posts on loan offers show only their introductions. Absence of a finding below means "not found in what could be read", not "does not exist".

Within those limits, what is known about how AI clubs decide comes to a short list. Officially, FM26's 26.1.0 update made "tweaks ... to ensure AI clubs only make offers they can afford" and improved "wage and transfer budget distribution logic for AI clubs" ([FM Scout copy of the 26.1.0 notes](https://www.fmscout.com/a-fm26-update-26.1.0.html)); AI managers since FM24 shortlist to fill gaps in their squad and to suit their tactics, weigh "form and reputation more so than ever before" and prefer potential for backup slots ([footballmanager.com, 19 Sep 2023](https://www.footballmanager.com/features/smarter-transfers-squad-building-and-finance)); and interest in a player is either "Active" or "Rumoured" ([footballmanager.com, 21 Sep 2023](https://www.footballmanager.com/features/introducing-intermediaries-and-offloading-players)). Those are carried over from FM26 and FM24, official, and none carries a number. The only tests are a tool author's, on FM26: FMSuperScout's regression of real in-game values on about 43 to 45 adult players, and its comparison of 55 players' stored values with the ranges the game displayed ([FMSuperScout value model](https://github.com/mavarobli/FMSuperScout/blob/main/docs/value-model.md); [calibration data](https://github.com/mavarobli/FMSuperScout/blob/main/tools/value-calib.js)). What has been datamined is the memory layout, not the decision: two open-source readers agree on where the value, the asking price and the reputations sit, and **nobody has published the game's valuation code, the numbers in the per-nation value table, or any threshold for a bid**. FM Scout's administrator said in 2017 that SI's "very complex dynamic calculation" of sale value "seems to be impossible to crack" ([FM Scout, 13 Feb 2017](https://www.fmscout.com/q-16658-Sale-Value.html)), and fm-arena's reverse-engineering threads cover training and the match engine, not transfers.

One FM27 claim deserves separate mention because it bears directly on the question and rests on one modder's word. The author of the "FM27 AI Value" plugin, working on a pre-release build, says the game values players at AI clubs differently from players at a human's club, that premiums for being a key player or not needing to sell go only to AI clubs, and that "the value you see on screen and the offers other clubs make for your players use the same calculation", with a user-set asking price still used first; his one worked example moved a CA 150 midfielder from 30.15M to 40.5M, about 34%, when the AI's valuation was applied ([FMPortugal, 6 Nov 2026](https://fmportugal.net/en/ai-value-fm27/)). That is FM27, anecdotal. If it is right, the on-screen value is the basis of a bid for the owner's player, not a floor under it.

## Selling: ask near stored value, in a shape the buyer's budget can carry

### Whether a buyer exists decides more than the price asked

No source for any version gives the fraction of value or asking price at which an AI club accepts when the human sells. The anecdotes agree only on direction, and the dividing line they draw is interest. On FM24, players that AI clubs were already pursuing sold "around or above their valuation" and clubs "often pay over the odds too", while players nobody wanted drew nothing: one could not be moved for eight months at 250k, "well under his value", and another user found no sale even "cutting down 50% value or offering zero" ([Steam FM24, Oct 2023](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2252570/discussions/0/3879346999824363743)). The same thread holds the belief that the AI, when it buys from the user, will "always offer you the bottom end of what a player is worth", staggered over time with conditions. Those are carried over from FM24, anecdotal, from a handful of saves at top-division clubs.

At the owner's level the numbers fall an order of magnitude. One FM24 manager reported, from a tier 6 English club, a sale at **£100K up front with £70K of add-ons and a 20% sell-on**, and from a tier 5 club a youth product sold to Cardiff for £100K with a 20% sell-on and a loan back; the same poster's players listed at £200K to £400K "moved for about 20k", roughly **5 to 10% of displayed value**, and one listed at £300K did not move even on a free ([Steam FM24, 22 Nov 2023](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2252570/discussions/0/4029094770946930030)). That is one poster and about three players. A single FM26 bug report describes TransferRoom offers at about 10% of value, after which the displayed value fell to the offer ([SI bug tracker, snippet only](https://community.sports-interactive.com/bugtracker/1644_football-manager-26-bugs-tracker/1883_transfer-scouting-contracts-intermediaries/potential-market-issue-or-bug-r42816/)); SI then treated both as faults, since 26.1.2 lists "Fixed an issue causing a players displayed value to drop drastically with no logical cause" and "When offering a player out via an Intermediary, users should receive more appropriate offers from AI Clubs" ([FM Scout copy of the 26.1.2 notes](https://www.fmscout.com/a-fm26-update-26.1.2.html)). Whether FM27 behaves like FM26 before or after that fix is untested. A poster in SI's FM27 feedback thread says AI clubs sometimes sign players from lower-league teams for less money after months of no interest ([SI feedback thread, snippet only](https://community.sports-interactive.com/forums/topic/612416-the-official-fm27-feedback-thread/page/51/)), which is FM27, anecdotal, and points the same way.

The FM27 starting budgets explain the lower-league discount without any "human tax", though this reading is speculation. On day one of a new save, as FM Scout published them on 1 November 2026 (most likely from the Launch Preview build; the page names none), the money is as follows ([FM Scout, FM27 starting transfer and wage budgets](https://www.fmscout.com/a-fm27-starting-transfer-and-wage-budgets.html)).

| Division | Transfer budget: min / max / median | Wage budget, £K a week: min / max / median |
|---|---|---|
| Championship | £0 / £25M / £875K | 199 / 1,331 / 388 |
| League One | £0 / £3M / £100K | 75 / 570 / 135 |
| League Two | £0 / £600K / £35K | 37 / 140 / 63.5 |
| National League | £0 / £200K / £10K | 20 / 63 / 30 |
| National League North | £0 / £10K / £0 | 5.7 / 26 / 12.5 |
| National League South | £0 / £30K / £0 | 9.4 / 27 / 14 |

In the National League one club of 24 has £100K or more, twelve have about £10K and seven have nothing; in National League North sixteen of 24 have nothing, and in the South twenty of 24. The wage budget not yet used on day one is £0 to £2K a week at most National League clubs and **£0 at 13 of 24 clubs in National League North**. A displayed value of £300K reflects reputation; a bid of £20K reflects a buyer who has £10K to £35K. The realistic payers of a five-figure fee are therefore League One, the richer half of League Two and above, and across the borders only the Scottish Premiership (median £225K), the Scottish Championship (£75K) and the League of Ireland Premier (£40K); the Cymru Premier and the lower Scottish and Irish divisions have medians of £0 to £35K (all medians here were worked out from the page's tables, and those outside England were not re-checked by hand), and no report was found of such a club bidding for an English non-league player in any version.

### What moves a bid, and what does not have a number

Every factor the owner asked about has directional support at best. The buyer's budget is the one hard limit with official backing (FM26, above). Existing active interest separates the two outcomes described (FM24, anecdotal). Transfer-listing is said to lower offers and to be "a last resort", and a set asking price is said to let the AI anchor on it, so that "Best is to leave it unspecified and let the offers come" ([FM Scout Q&A, version unknown](https://www.fmscout.com/q-23770-Getting-UnderPaid-for-Players.html)); FMSuperScout's FM26 check found that for a transfer-listed player the stored value field was the club's asking price in 4 of 4 cases, within 1% ([FMSuperScout app.js](https://github.com/mavarobli/FMSuperScout/blob/main/app/app.js)), which is carried over from FM26, tested on a tiny sample, and fits the plugin author's "a user-set asking price is still used first". The agent chat offers three stances, "Desperate", "Keen" and "Curious", the last "if you're willing to sell but only at the right price", and SI does not say how each changes the fee ([footballmanager.com, 21 Sep 2023](https://www.footballmanager.com/features/introducing-intermediaries-and-offloading-players)).

Contract length needs care. The repository's FM26 notes carry a claim that AI clubs cut offers by 50 to 70% in the last twelve months of a contract; its source is a Reddit thread that could not be read ([the thread it cites](https://www.reddit.com/r/footballmanager/comments/1okg83o/release_clause_is_the_most_stupid_annoying_thing/)), and it has no test. What is tested, on FM26, is that the stored value itself already falls with the contract: in FMSuperScout's fit, value scales with months left to the power 0.507, so twelve months left gives about 57% of the value at 36 months and six months about 40%, with a further ×0.7 at four months or fewer ([FMSuperScout app.js](https://github.com/mavarobli/FMSuperScout/blob/main/app/app.js)). A calculator that anchors on stored value and then applies a final-year discount would count the contract twice; this report's algorithm does not apply one. Nothing at all was found, for a human seller, on the effect of age, of the player's reputation against the buyer's, of the selling club's reputation, of unhappiness or a transfer request, or of the day in the window. FM27 brought Deadline Day back and says agent offers "ramp up as it gets to the business end of the window" ([footballmanager.com, FM27 feature page](https://www.footballmanager.com/fm27/features/deeper-more-immersive-world)), but those are players offered to the user; nothing official says AI clubs bid more for a human's players late.

### Deal structure: the 20% sell-on is the only attested figure

No source gives the weight an AI buyer puts on an instalment, an add-on, a sell-on, a buy-back or a friendly, and no test shows a refused cash offer accepted at the same total once restructured. The evidence that structure matters is indirect. On FM24 the AI as seller accepted £0 up front, six instalments of £200, £2.5M after 50 appearances and a 10% sell-on for a world-class player, which shows it counted a distant appearance add-on at close to face value ([FourFourTwo, 14 Feb 2024](https://fourfourtwo.com/news/football-manager-2024-heres-how-to-sign-any-player-for-just-pound25m-fm24)); that is the other side of the deal, in an older version, and whether it was patched was not confirmed. Both reported non-league sales carried a **20% sell-on**, and one had add-ons worth 70% of the cash fee. FM27's own Director of Football, in SI's livestream of 22 October 2026, advised on a purchase of a transfer-listed striker: a ceiling of £25.5M, an opening total of about £19M, made of £13M up front, £2.6M in three instalments, £3.4M after 50 league appearances and 20% of the profit from the next sale ([FMPortugal, 25 Oct 2026](https://fmportugal.net/en/fm27-diretor-desportivo-negociacoes/)). By this report's arithmetic that opening is 68% cash, 14% instalments and 18% appearance money, and the ceiling is 1.34 times the opening. It is one example, for a purchase by the human's club, and the article does not say whether the seller accepted; but it is the only FM27 picture of the shape the game itself proposes, and its sell-on is again 20%.

FM27's sell-on change is narrower than it is often summarised. SI's words are that a sell-on "will now be surfaced in Scout Reports" and that "clubs will want more money for those players to ensure that they don't miss out financially" ([footballmanager.com, FM27 feature page](https://www.footballmanager.com/fm27/features/deeper-more-immersive-world)): an AI club that owes a sell-on asks more when it sells. SI gives no size and says nothing about a buyer discounting its bid for a sell-on the human asks for. The one figure in circulation, that a 10% sell-on knocks 15 to 20% off a fee, comes from an unsigned FM27 wiki page dated before release with no test ([footballmanagers27.wiki](https://footballmanagers27.wiki/guides/fm27-transfers)) and should not be coded. Guides name 50% as the sell-on to ask for on young players, with no report of it being accepted ([VideoGamer](https://www.videogamer.com/?p=1139872)). One point is plain arithmetic: at the same percentage, a share of the next fee is never less than a share of the profit, so the owner should ask for the fee, and old forum advice says to exclude percentage-of-profit early and ask for a plain sell-on ([fm-arena](https://fm-arena.com/thread/5544-players-negotiation/)). Reports on selling a sell-on clause back later are mixed: one FM24 poster recommends it and the thread's starter found clubs often would not buy it ([Steam FM24, Feb 2025](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2252570/discussions/0/591763911730069212)).

### Negotiation and routes: counter once, and count the commission

There is no PC-version data on how far an AI club rises from its first bid. A Steam poster on FM24 says "Sometimes they'll budge slightly, but it's normally up to like, 10-20% above what they initially offered", and that past an unknown threshold, "sometimes 0 wiggle room", they pull out ([Steam FM24, Jan 2025](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2252570/discussions/0/603020257754360005/)). The only worked numbers are from FM22 Mobile, a different product: two sales that closed at 2.0 and 5.6 times the first bid over five offers, with the fourth reply "very close to the final price" ([FMM Vibe, 26 Jan 2022](https://fmmvibe.com/forums/topic/47312-how-to-sell-players-for-negotiation-techniques/)). An SI forum post from the FM22 to FM24 era describes "2-3 chances to make them accept your offer before they either pull out or give you a non-negotiable offer" ([SI forum, snippet only](https://community.sports-interactive.com/forums/topic/567248-change-the-way-transfer-and-contract-negotiations-work)). With several bidders, a Substack post from the FM24 era advises accepting none but the highest, rejecting the rest, and offering the player out again one step higher, which can "sometimes more than double the original amount offered"; it shows no data ([Football Manager Projects, 12 Jan 2024](https://fmprojects.substack.com/p/starting-a-bidding-war-for-your-player-0b6)). FM27 puts all offers for one player in a single message ([FM Scout, 21 Oct 2026](https://www.fmscout.com/a-fm27-transfers.html)), which makes that comparison easier.

On routes, an intermediary takes a commission "capped at a maximum of 10% of the overall transfer fee" and finds offers faster, and rejecting a deal an intermediary brought puts off that intermediary and club for a short time; TransferRoom "is not an instant option" (all carried over from FM24, official: [footballmanager.com, 21 Sep 2023](https://www.footballmanager.com/features/introducing-intermediaries-and-offloading-players); [FM Scout, 22 Sep 2023](https://www.fmscout.com/a-fm24-offloading-players.html)). Neither is documented as raising the fee. "Ask agent about market interest" returns "the player's current market value and the clubs most interested", and the price tag on his profile updates to that figure for a time; SI never calls it the fee that will be bid. In FM27, 27.1.0 fixed a fault that stopped a player being offered again through TransferRoom Out after drawing no interest ([FM Scout, 27.1.0 notes](https://www.fmscout.com/a-fm27-update-27.1.0.html)), so pitching can now be repeated. Whether a TransferRoom requirement shows a fee or wage ceiling was not found; the owner should look at a requirement card.

Three rules at this level limit who can be sold at all. A non-contract or short-term player cannot be loaned and has no sale price, since any club can take him ([SI forum, snippet only](https://community.sports-interactive.com/forums/topic/592561-can%E2%80%99t-offer-player-for-loan/)). A player who signed within the last six months is reported as unsellable and unloanable ([Steam FM18](https://steamcommunity.com/app/624090/discussions/0/1752358461531539277)), an old anecdote with nothing newer to confirm it. And a player under 24 who leaves at the end of his contract brings compensation set by a tribunal: one FM24 user was charged "basically two years salary" for such a signing ([Steam FM24, Nov 2024](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2252570/discussions/0/4625853246786831803/)). If the game charges that for the owner's contract types, which no source confirms for part-time or youth terms, then a £300-a-week player under 24 carries a floor near £31K, more than most tier 5 and 6 clubs can pay as a fee.

## Loans out: the rule is value ÷ 48 a month, and it rarely supports a fee here

### The correction to the earlier file

`docs/Research/fm27-selling-and-loans.md`, in its section Q5, said until this research: "if his wage is below value ÷ 12, ask 100% wage plus a monthly fee". **That wording is wrong by a factor of four.** The article it cites sets out the sum itself: "Take the player's value, or the lowest figure in his value range for more modern games. Divide that number by 12." and then "take that number and divide it by 4. This will give you his value per month." ([Football Manager Projects, 4 Nov 2023](https://fmprojects.substack.com/p/how-to-loan-players-out-for-profit)). So the borrower is assumed to tolerate wages plus fee of **value ÷ 48 a month**, which is 25% of value a year, and the shortcut is to compare the weekly wage with **value ÷ 192**. If four times the weekly wage is less than value ÷ 48, ask 100% of wages plus the difference as a monthly fee, the same for playing and non-playing; otherwise ask the nearest wage percentage rounded down to a multiple of 10, and no fee. The article's examples: value $6M and $25,000 a week give 125,000 against 100,000, so 100% wages plus $25,000 a month; value $3M and the same wage give 62.5%, so ask 60%. That line was corrected on 22 November 2026, with a note pointing here.

The rule's standing is weak. Its author calls it "a theory about how Football Manager's AI assigns value to players", traces it to "rumors from YouTube videos" and two Reddit comments (neither readable), names no FM version, reports no sample, and says it "works more often than not on aggregate". It is carried over from about FM22, anecdotal. Other anecdotes do not fit it: on FM14 no club would pay more than 200k a month for players valued 3.2M and just over 10M ([FM Scout, Sep 2014](https://www.fmscout.com/q-13423-Cant-get-more-than-200K-a-month-as-part-of-a-loan-deal.html)), three times value ÷ 48 for the first, while on FM24 a goalkeeper valued €85–93M drew 100% wages plus €100K a month playing, a small fraction of it ([Steam FM24, Feb 2024](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2252570/discussions/0/4306075118776965880)). Which "value" the AI uses is also unknown; the rule says the low end of the range, which by the tool evidence below is the stored value.

Applied at tiers 5 to 6 the rule nearly always gives a part share of wages and no fee. A player valued £50,000 on £500 a week has value ÷ 192 of £260 a week, 52% of his wage, so the ask is 50% and no fee. A fee appears only when value is more than 192 times the weekly wage, which for a £500-a-week player means a value above £96,000.

### Wage share: propose it, and cap it at the borrower's wage room

AI clubs open low and can be moved by the human's own proposal. On FM24 one user reported that AI clubs "will only make a 0% squad player offer but you open offer set it to 100% and regular starter or higher and they accept", and that preset loan conditions cost offers because "the ai starts with lower offers so if you set them there you miss out on a ton of offers" ([Steam FM24, Oct 2023](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2252570/discussions/0/3879346999818738725)). That is carried over from FM24, anecdotal, with no count. The one non-league observation is older and from the other side: a National League North manager on FM20 noted that "most of the players I see in this league signed on loan after the game started are on 0% wage loans" ([FM Scout, Nov 2019](https://www.fmscout.com/q-20345-Loans-in-LLM.html)). With thirteen of 24 clubs in that division starting FM27 with no free wage budget, 0% is what the budgets predict.

The official constraint is again affordability, from FM26's 26.1.0 (above), together with "AI clubs will now set more realistic goals during negotiations for loan players". An FM19 Steam answer, which an SI developer in the thread did not contradict, gives the same two causes for no interest: a contract signed within the last six months, or "the other club cannot afford his wages", in which case offer to cover part of them ([Steam FM19, Jan 2019](https://steamcommunity.com/app/872790/discussions/0/1741106440022096853)). The FM26 Mobile manual says the wage share moves "in 10% increments" and is restricted by the borrower's "current Maximum Wage" ([SI manual, snippet only](https://community.sports-interactive.com/sigames-manual/football-manager-mobile-2026/transfers-r5258/)); whether desktop FM27 applies that cap to AI borrowers is unverified, and a third-party FM27 tool's own loan sheet uses 5% steps ([MacLibero RT](https://maclibero.com/blog/loan-release-player/)), so the step on FM27's own screen needs one look. Nothing ties the accepted share to the borrower's budget with numbers, and no lower-league sample of human-proposed shares exists in any version.

### Playing time, recall and future fees

Playing time is a term the AI weighs when it accepts, and nothing shows it honours it. The FM24 anecdote above has clubs accepting "regular starter or higher"; an SI forum poster says "clubs will not honor negotiated playing time" ([SI forum, snippet only](https://community.sports-interactive.com/forums/topic/555024-loaning-out-players-playing-time/); a human borrower describing himself); and guides advise asking "at least a Regular Starter" for a development loan while keeping "the option to recall the player if necessary" ([Passion4FM, Oct 2025](https://www.passion4fm.com/using-loans-to-improve-youth-development-on-football-manager/)). No test gives an acceptance rate by playing-time level. FM27 adds monthly reports on young players out on loan and makes AI managers "a little less strict" about recalling their own loanees ([FM Scout, 21 Oct 2026](https://www.fmscout.com/a-fm27-transfers.html)); the first is the owner's means of checking a promise, the second concerns loans into his club. Old reports say a recall clause cannot be combined with a loan fee: "Recall for injuries: ticking this means no fee" on FM19 ([Steam FM19](https://steamcommunity.com/app/872790/discussions/0/1741106440022096853)), and the game's own warning quoted from FM11, "You can't use this clause if you're receiving a loan fee" ([SI forum, snippet only](https://community.sigames.com/forums/topic/200997-need-more-power-when-it-comes-to-loaning-players-out/)). Whether FM24 to FM27 still enforce that is unconfirmed. At this level, where a fee is rarely supportable, keeping the recall costs little either way. Nothing was found on how "can play against owner" or cup eligibility change acceptance.

On future fees, the official record says only that the AI uses them more since FM26 ("More loans with future fees and buyback clauses", [footballmanager.com](https://www.footballmanager.com/fm26/features/powered-transferroom-fm26s-recruitment-revamp)) and that 26.1.0 stopped AI clubs adding them for players with under twelve months of contract. An FM22 bug report says AI clubs "most often respond with non negotiable loan offers including future fees for high-potential players" when a loan is offered round ([SI bug tracker, snippet only](https://community.sports-interactive.com/bugtracker/previous-versions/football-manager-2022-bugs-forum/football-manager-2022-early-access-beta-bugs-forum/transfers-contracts-scouting-recruitment-meetings-and-staff-responsibilities/transfer-offer-to-clubs-loan-offer-out-unable-to-tell-clubs-that-you-are-unwilling-to-accept-any-offers-that-include-a-future-fee-r3491)). No source gives the fee an AI club accepts as an optional or mandatory future fee, or says that adding one makes a loan easier to place. For a prospect the owner wants back, the safe reading is to refuse an optional fee; for a wage he wants gone, an optional fee is lower-risk than a mandatory one, which needs the player to agree a contract with the borrower first ([FM Scout, FM20](https://www.fmscout.com/q-20209-Mandatory-or-future-fee.html?p=262628)).

### League rules cap who can borrow, and the game's own numbers were not found

No source states FM26's or FM27's own loan limits, windows or registration rules for the National League and below, and 27.1.0 changed none of them. In-game anecdotes from earlier versions match the real rulebook: "6 long-term loans per season, and a slot is used up as soon as the loan is made" ([SI forum, about FM19, snippet only](https://community.sports-interactive.com/forums/topic/453747-loan-rules-for-english-national-league/)); a deal refused on FM24 because more than two players on loan from one club "breaks the National League's rules on loans" ([SI bug tracker, snippet only, one report](https://community.sports-interactive.com/bugtracker/football-manager-2024-early-access-bugs-tracker/transfers-scouting-contracts-and-agentsintermediaries/loan-back-w-too-many-loans-perma-stops-transfer-r21317)); and under-21 loans that "don't count towards the long-term loan limit" ([SI forum, snippet only](https://community.sports-interactive.com/forums/topic/516913-vanarama-loan-rules/)). The real rules, which are not in-game evidence, allow six long-term loans, two to or from any one club at a time, five loan players in a match-day squad, a 28-day minimum, and unlimited youth loans for players of 20 or under on 1 July ([DAZN](https://www.dazn.com/en-CA/news/soccer/national-league-january-transfer-window/13umjky2mmhs21mkfh8plar15a); [FA Standardised Rules 2024-25](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/files/thefaportal/governance-docs/rules-of-the-association/2024-25/february-2025-update/29---standardised-rules.ashx)). Real-world sources disagree on whether non-league loans need an open window (DAZN says the National League has none; FA guidance says a long-term loan "can only be authorised during an open Transfer Window", [FA Player Status](https://playerstatus.thefa.com/support/solutions/articles/80001135185-process-to-contract-or-loan-a-player)), and no source states the game's behaviour, so nothing is known about timing. If the game copies the rules, a slot spent early in the season is gone, borrowers have most slots at the start of summer, and a player of 20 or under is easier to place than one of 21 to 23; all three are speculation. The competition's Rules screen in the owner's save is the only place found to hold the game's numbers.

## Hidden values: what each is, where it is read, and what it bounds

Two open-source memory readers, read on 22 November 2026, agree on the player fields for FM27. FMSuperScout's field map, "verified 02-11-2026 against a running FM 27.0 save and 20-11-2026 against FM 27.1 (all field offsets unchanged)", places two adjacent money fields in pounds and three reputations (home, current, world, each 0 to 10,000) in the player block ([FMSuperScout Fields.cs](https://github.com/mavarobli/FMSuperScout/blob/main/core/Fields.cs)); FMLibreEditor independently describes the first as "his transfer value, which the game shows in the player's currency as a range starting at it while his club's scouts do not know him well; 300,000,000 when he is not for sale", followed by the asking price ([FMLibreEditor profile.rs](https://github.com/tozmoz/FMLibreEditor/blob/HEAD/crates/fmlibre-core/src/profile.rs)). Those are FM27, tool-documented. The stored value is thus the low end of the range on screen, or close to it: FMSuperScout's FM26 comparison of 55 players found the displayed range "encloses our value field nearly always within about ±15%", with the high end about 1.1 to 1.3 times the low end for well-known players and exactly ten times for obscure ones, and a single figure where the club has fixed an asking price.

The only published formula for value is FMSuperScout's FM26 fit: value follows **world reputation** (about ×2.7 for each 1,000 points, flattening above 7,500), falls about 6.7% for each year of age and scales with roughly the square root of months left, and "FM value mainly follows world reputation (fame), not CA". It has no nation or league term and was fitted on Dutch and world-class players, so its constant overshoots badly at non-league level (it gives about £230K for a 25-year-old with reputation 1,500 and two years left); its slopes are the usable part. An FM21 mod author describes the per-nation Transfer Values table in the pre-game editor, keyed on reputation ranges, in which "A specific player's value can't be changed" ([FM Scout, 16 Jan 2021](https://www.fmscout.com/a-fm21-realistic-transfer-market-values.html)); the table's numbers have never been posted, and sources disagree on which of the three reputations drives it. No source isolates position, wage or league reputation as inputs.

On the club side FMRTE 27 documents everything a calculator would want and nothing on how the AI uses it. Its club Finances overview shows Balance, Transfer Budget (Season), Transfer Budget Remaining, Wage Budget, Wage Used, "Max Wage (aprox.)" as an approximate limit for one wage offer, "% of transfer revenue made available", any transfer embargo, and debts ([docs.fmrte.com, Club finances](https://docs.fmrte.com/clubs/finances/)), and its Advanced Search has a Club type with ranges for balance, both transfer budgets and the wage budget ([docs.fmrte.com, Search](https://docs.fmrte.com/scouting/search/)). FMRTE has no page that shows what the game would accept: its Move to club form "starts at the player's asking price" and hands the game an already accepted deal, so it runs none of the AI's evaluation and **cannot be used to test acceptance** ([docs.fmrte.com, Transfers](https://docs.fmrte.com/players/transfers/)).

The `Value` column in his `FutureTransfers` grid is the agreed fee of a done deal, as FMRTE's documentation says ("Value is the fee"), not the player's valuation. FM27's Squad Cost Ratio is described by SI only as "the introduction of Squad Cost Ratio rules and readjustments to global spending"; the 85% and 70% figures in circulation are the real Premier League and UEFA rules, nothing says the game applies any such rule at tiers 4 to 7, and it should not be modelled.

## An algorithm of labelled guesses, and the tests that replace them

What follows is this report's proposal. Its structure rests on the directional evidence above; **none of its numbers has an FM27 test behind it**, and in the engine's terms every one would be labelled derived or carried over, never measured.

**The sale** starts with a gate: a non-contract or short-term player gets no fee, a player on a youth contract can only bring compensation, and one who joined within six months is reported as unsellable. Next comes the market check, which outputs words, not a number: with no club showing active interest and no TransferRoom requirement he fits, the advice is to create interest first (agent chat as "Curious", a pitch against a requirement, then an intermediary) and to expect bids at 5 to 10% of displayed value if he is simply offered round. For each candidate buyer the calculator then reads three ceilings from FMRTE: cash at most the Transfer Budget Remaining, the wage he would be offered at most the free wage room, and at most the Max Wage. The anchor is the agent's market value when the chat has been held, else the stored value; the target total is 1.0 times the anchor where interest is active. The cash part is the smaller of the target and the buyer's budget, and the remainder goes into instalments and an add-on after league appearances, with a 20% sell-on of the next fee on top, asked for more firmly the younger the player and the more the buyer sells on. When a bid arrives the first is never accepted; one counter goes back at 1.2 times its total, adding to the structured part before the cash; a non-negotiable bid is weighed against the walk-away value, which is the larger of the best bid in hand, what he is worth to the squad for his remaining months, and any compensation due at the end of his contract. Routes are compared on fee times one minus commission. A League Two buyer with £35K to spend and a player whose stored value is £40K would thus be asked for £35K now, £5K after appearances and 20% of the next fee; a National League club with £10K would be asked for £10K now and £30K deferred, and whether an AI club accepts deferred money beyond its budget is exactly what is not known.

**The loan** has the same gate (permanent contract, not signed within six months) and a borrower filter: a free long-term loan slot and fewer than two players already on loan from the owner, read off the game's Rules screen and the borrower's squad; free wage room above zero; and a gap at his position. The weekly tolerance is stored value ÷ 192. The wage share asked is the smallest of 100%, tolerance ÷ weekly wage, free wage room ÷ weekly wage and Max Wage ÷ weekly wage, rounded down to the step. A monthly fee, the same for playing and non-playing, is asked only when the share is 100% and value ÷ 48 exceeds four times the weekly wage, and is that difference. A development loan for a player of 18 to 23 asks Regular Starter, keeps the recall, allows cup matches, refuses a future fee and gives up wage share before playing time, because what the owner is buying is matches. A loan to shed a wage asks the highest share that passes, one playing-time level lower, and allows an optional future fee. After a refusal the ask steps down one wage step, in the old order "wages full, wages half, wages free" ([Steam FM16](https://steamcommunity.com/app/378120/discussions/0/385428458162266706)); after several quick acceptances the next player's ask goes up.

## Conclusion

The search for a formula ends in a different kind of answer: the game's decision is unpublished, but nearly every input to it is readable, so the owner is better placed to measure it than anyone who has written about it. The evidence that exists points one way throughout. A displayed value at tiers 5 to 6 is a statement about reputation, a bid is a statement about a budget of £0 to £35K, and the terms worth proposing are the ones that fit inside the second: little cash, deferred and conditional money, a share of the next sale, and for loans a wage share the borrower's free wage room can carry. The same reading says that the single most useful datum, the stored transfer value, is the one his current FMRTE paste lacks, and that the buyer's three finance figures sit on a page he has not yet pasted from.

For the tool, that argues for building the calculator and the logbook together. Each proposal the owner makes, with its terms, the buyer's budget figures, the game version and the answer, is a data point that no forum holds, and a dozen of them would let the guessed multipliers above be replaced by measured ones with an honest evidence label. Until then the advice should be shown as what it is: a starting offer and a reason, with "this tool's estimate" on every number and no promise that a club will say yes.
