# FM27 training intensity by condition

Researched 21 November 2026, eleven days after release and four after patch 27.1.0. Labels: (a) official SI or the game's own screen, (b) FM27 test with data, (c) FM26 or earlier, carried over, (d) opinion or guess. "(b), no figures" means an FM27 tester's statement with no numbers shown.

## Recommendation

No FM27 test has varied intensity band by band, so no table here rests on a measurement of the bands themselves. Two tables are defensible. For this squad I would set the first.

| Condition band | Freshness (my pick) | Weakest label | Development (the testers' table) | Weakest label |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Very poor | No pitch or gym work | (c) | No pitch or gym work | (c) |
| Poor | No pitch or gym work | (c) | No pitch or gym work | (c) |
| Fair | No pitch or gym work | (c) | No pitch or gym work | (c) |
| Good | Half intensity | (b), no figures | Double intensity | (d) |
| Excellent | Normal intensity | (d) | Double intensity | (c) for the gain; cost unmeasured |

Set with it:

1. **A full rest day (all sessions Rest) the day after every match.** After patch 27.1.0 the game may put a recovery session or individual sessions into that day; look at each kind of week once and take them out (b, no figures, for the benefit; (b) for the patch behaviour).
2. **Training packed into as few days as possible, the other days full rest.** Injuries are rolled only on days with training: 13.8% to 4.0% in a 36-day FM27 test, with almost identical development (b, second-hand). Each such day costs 0.5% sharpness (b).
3. **Individual Intensity left alone for every player.** A player given his own intensity no longer follows the bands (a). Do not let the assistant run training (b).
4. **The U18 and U21 squads' Training Intensity screens checked once.** Whether the bands are shared between squads: no evidence found.

## 1. The FM27 screen

The setting is on **Training, Training Intensity**. The top part is "Team Intensity", with the line "Automatically set training intensity and rest based on player condition" and five drop-downs, one for each of Very poor, Poor, Fair, Good and Excellent condition (a, ZaZ's screenshot of 10 November 2026). Each takes one of four values: No pitch or gym work, Half Intensity, Normal Intensity, Double Intensity. Under it the screen lists upcoming fixtures, and then "Individual Intensity": "Setting individual player training intensity will override the automatic intensity selection", with a row for each player showing training happiness, condition, sharpness, injury risk, a physio recommendation and the intensity he is on.

**The default.** One guide (allthings.how, 19 November 2026) gives it as No pitch or gym work for Very poor, Half for Poor and Normal for Fair, Good and Excellent. It is a guide site and shows no screenshot of the defaults, so this is (d) until read off the game. If it is right, a player trains at Normal on every training day once he is back to Fair, which fits the load the owner sees.

**What the bands mean in percent.** No evidence found for FM27. The percentages the screen used before hearts replaced them (FM19, FM20), repeated in an FM24 guide, are 59% or under, 60 to 69, 70 to 79, 80 to 89 and 90 and over (c, from a guide, not checked against the game). ZaZ speaks of Good as "80% or so" (d). If that mapping still holds, the line between Good and Excellent is about 90%. It can be checked in a minute: the Individual Intensity list shows each player's heart, and an external reader shows the number on the same day.

**Per squad.** No evidence found. Schedules are per squad (jamiebolden publishes separate senior and U18 schedules), and the screen sits under one squad's Training menu, so each squad's screen should be looked at.

**Rest sessions.** The screen's own words are that the bands set "intensity and rest". ZaZ describes the full recovery after a match as two things together, "full rest + send home to rest" (15 November 2026). An FM26 fm-arena post, seen only as a search engine's summary, says that no pitch or gym work on the bands below full condition "will do the super rest automatically" (c, not read in full). So the band setting supplies the "sent home" half and the schedule supplies the rest day. Whether a Rest session ignores the band setting, or the reverse: no evidence found.

**Team-wide intensity.** There is no separate team-wide intensity on the screenshot; "Team Intensity" is the row of bands. The schedule's own load indicators (a, SI's FM27 training article) are a separate display.

**Patch 27.1.0.** Its change list names nothing on this screen (a). What changed is the schedule: see section 5.

## 2. Tests of intensity by condition

Nobody has published an FM27 test that varies the intensity at one band and measures the result. What exists:

| Measure | No pitch or gym work | Half | Normal | Double |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Condition recovered a day | No FM27 figure. FM26: "an enormous amount" the day after a match, no numbers (c) | No evidence found | FM27: lower match-day condition than Half, no figures (b) | FM27: lower match-day condition than Half, no figures (b) |
| Jadedness a day | No FM27 figure. FM24: three rest days cleared 101 to 152; with general training 57 to 69 (c) | No evidence found | No evidence found | No evidence found |
| Injury risk in training | None rolled on a day with no training (b, ZaZ and the 36-day test) | No evidence found | "feeds the game's injury risk" (b, no figures) | Game's label "Increased" (b, one screenshot); 999 against 499 days in FM26 (c, unchecked) |
| Sharpness | −0.5% a day with no training, none the day after a match (b). Whether a player on no pitch work on a training day loses it: no evidence found | No evidence found | No evidence found | No evidence found |
| Growth | The day's training is probably lost (d, Harvestgreen22's own guess in FM26) | No evidence found | Baseline | +1.7 CA a year over Normal (c, FM24) |

What FM27 does measure is the size of training as a whole, at Double for Good and Excellent and nothing below: an 18-year-old gained 10.7 CA in a season with no training and 22 to 33 with it (b, Harvestgreen22, injuries off, top facilities, two matches a week). And it measures what more training days cost: injury days rose from 362 to 846 as tactical sessions went from none to 24 a month, and to 1,072 when the assistant ran training (b, jamiebolden, one club).

The FM24 finding that "training schedules barely change development" does not sit easily beside Harvestgreen22's FM27 numbers. The FM24 result is a harness at a fixed 100 match points a month and is about match value, not CA; the FM27 result is in the game. jamiebolden's own reading of the FM27 test is that training directs growth and does not cause it, which his table of 10.7 against 33.3 does not obviously support. Both have data; they measure different things.

## 4. The trade-off for young players

- **Half or no pitch work against Normal or Double, ages 16 to 21: no evidence found**, in any version. The only measured step is Double over Normal, +1.7 CA a year in FM24 for players of 17 to 23 (c).
- Against that, training as a whole is worth 12 to 22 CA a season to an 18-year-old in FM27 (b). So the sessions matter far more than the intensity they are done at. Half at Good keeps the sessions; no pitch work at Good gives them up on every day a player is not at Excellent, which for a slow recoverer could be most of the week (d, my reasoning).
- A light schedule "captures most of the gains" (b, the unnamed tester's comment), and U18 growth was the same to within 5% across four very different set-ups (b, jamiebolden).
- Under-19s get a floor of match experience without playing, and 16 to 18 is the age of fastest growth (c, FM24 code reading in jamiebolden's `docs/match_experience.md`). A 16-year-old who starts every week is already past the floor; what he can lose is matches, through injury or tiredness.
- **What Double costs:** injuries, 999 against 499 days in FM26 (c, unchecked) and the game's "Increased" label in FM27 (b, one screenshot); match-day condition in FM27 (b, no figures); jadedness, no evidence found. Condition is the largest effect FM27's match tests have found (80% costs 0.8 points a game, b), so a point or two of CA a year is a poor trade for it in a squad that is already arriving short.

## 5. What goes with it in the schedule

- **The day after a match: full rest**, not a recovery session. ZaZ (FM27, no figures) says it is never worth skipping; the FM26 and FM24 tests in the repository's notes put rest sessions ahead of recovery sessions (c). That day costs no sharpness (b).
- **After patch 27.1.0 the game edits rest days.** A full rest day in a week without a match gets two individual sessions, and a day after a match gets a recovery session (b, ZaZ, 17 November 2026). One user's fix is to delete the match from the schedule template and leave the day as rest (post 64). A schedule made before 17 November should be opened and checked, for the one-match week and the two-match week.
- **Few training days.** Injuries are rolled only on training days (b). The price is 0.5% sharpness for each day without training other than the day after a match (b); eight training days a month keep a tactic's training contribution topped up (b, ZaZ).
- **The day before a match: no evidence found** for a rest day there in FM27.
- **What stops the bands applying:** an individual intensity set on a player (a). Whether individual training workload, a Rest session or the assistant's "set all training intensities" changes them: no evidence found, except that assistant-run training as a whole did badly (b).
- **Individual rest by hand** is not needed if the bottom bands are on no pitch or gym work (c).

## Why the freshness table for this squad

1. Starters at 90 to 92% three days after a match, and players who did not start gaining jadedness, are what Normal from Fair upward would produce. The table differs from the reported default in three cells: Poor and Fair go to no work, Good goes to Half.
2. It corrects itself: a slow recoverer spends longer in Good and so trains at Half for longer, without anyone opening the screen.
3. What it gives up is measured and small (Double's 1.7 CA a year in FM24). What it protects is the largest measured match effect in FM27.
4. The case for Double at Good is one tester's unmeasured view, formed at a top club with the best facilities, and his own advice for squads with poor depth is to favour rest.

If development is to be pushed, the least costly step is Double at Excellent only, leaving Good at Half; a player then trains hard only when he has fully recovered. Its cost in FM27 is unmeasured.
