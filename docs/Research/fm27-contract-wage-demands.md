# Wage demands in FM27: no formula is published, so a reasonable range can rest only on the club's own budget, its own squad and the agent's quote

Researched on 25 November 2026, with FM27 at 27.1.0.

Nobody has published a test, a datamine or a Sports Interactive statement, for FM27 or any earlier version, that gives the wage a player asks for as a function of his ability, his reputation, his value or his division. FM27 itself is silent: the two contract lines in the 27.1.0 notes concern a release clause and a freeze. What is official comes from FM26 and is carried over: the game holds a **desired wage** for each player and a **maximum wage the club can offer**, compares the two before talks open, and had its "logic behind player wage demands in the lower leagues" changed in 26.0.6 in a direction the notes do not state. Everything else is anecdote, and the anecdotes agree on three things: the demand follows the club and the offer more than the player (the wage budget available, the playing time agreed, what other players are paid), it jumps at promotion (one report of about £1.5K a week in the National League and about £15K a week after promotion to League Two), and it moves between the estimate shown and the talks themselves. No tool exposes the demand: FMRTE 27, FMSuperScout and FMLibreEditor document the wage paid, never a wage asked. The one number the game does give is the agent's own, in the "Ask Agent About Availability" chat and on the contract screen, and that is the best input the owner can type in. A range the tool shows can therefore honestly rest on four things only: (1) FMRTE's "Max Wage (aprox.)" and the wage budget left, as hard limits; (2) a share of the wage budget by squad role, which is an official-site author's rule of thumb and not a mechanic (7.5% of the budget for each of four key players, 4.3% for each of seven starters, 2.7% for each of eleven backups); (3) what the owner's own players in the same tier are paid now; and (4) the agent's quote set against those. This report's own measurement on the repository's captures argues against the obvious version of (3): among 27 full-time players of the owner's squad, the wage paid has a rank correlation of 0.11 with current ability and 0.06 with world reputation, and among 49 paid players at other clubs, all of ability 60 to 75, it has none at all, with wages spread nineteenfold. A range read off "players of similar ability" is not supported; a range read off the squad's wage distribution by tier is the most that the data carry. The Sports Interactive forums, bug tracker and manual, Sortitoutsi, FM Inside and inkl could not be read, Reddit and YouTube were not reached, and about twenty logged negotiations in the owner's own save would be the first measured sample that exists in public.

## No test in any version gives a wage as a function of the player

This report keeps five kinds of claim apart. **Tested** means someone compared numbers and published the comparison. **Official** means Sports Interactive said it, in a feature page, a hints page, an article on its site or patch notes (where a patch line reached these notes through a news site's copy, it is marked secondary). **Tool-documented** means the documentation or source code of FMRTE 27, FMSuperScout or FMLibreEditor says a field exists or is shown. **Anecdotal** means a forum post or guide with no data, and **speculation** means an inference made in the research or in this report. Anything from before FM27 is marked "carried over from" its version. There is **no finding labelled "FM27, tested"** below, and none labelled "tested" for any version on what a player demands. The only numbers measured for this report are its own, on wages paid in the repository's three captures, and they are marked as that.

What could not be read matters as much as what was. **Sports Interactive's forum returned HTTP 403** on the one thread tried, as in the earlier research, so every SI forum, bug-tracker and manual item below was seen only as a search engine's summary of the page and is marked "snippet only". **inkl** ("The art of negotiating in Football Manager 26"), **Sortitoutsi** (its FM26 guides on buying players and on assessing a contract), **FM Inside** (its wage cap guide), FM Scout's old manual page on offering a contract and one Operation Sports article returned 403. Two fm-arena pages returned 404, among them a profile on which a user asks whether anyone has researched "how wage demand is calculated"; no answer to it was found. footballhandbook.com's guide to reducing wage demands now redirects to an unrelated site, afkgaming's FM24 negotiation guide redirects to its front page, and footballmanager.com/node/665 served the site's hub in place of the article. **Reddit and YouTube** were not fetched and appeared in no search result, so a video test with no write-up cannot be ruled out. No FM27 forum thread on wage demands was found on Steam or FM Scout; the game was 15 days old. Pages were read through a fetching tool that returns extracts, so quotations are as it returned them, and two patch lines came back cut short. Absence of a finding means "not found in what could be read", not "does not exist".

Three searches for something quantitative came back empty and are findings in themselves. FM Scout's administrator wrote in 2011 that "you can't possibly search by wage demand because it's a very unstable factor that changes per team and depends on random stuff" ([FM Scout, 9 Dec 2011](https://www.fmscout.com/q-4090-Search-for-free-agents-by-wage-demand-limit.html); carried over from FM12, anecdotal, but from the author of Genie Scout). The two open-source memory readers hold the weekly wage of the contract and of an offer and nothing named or described as a demand ([FMSuperScout Fields.cs](https://github.com/mavarobli/FMSuperScout/blob/main/core/Fields.cs), `CON_WEEKLY_WAGE`, "u32 GBP p/w"; [FMLibreEditor profile.rs](https://github.com/tozmoz/FMLibreEditor/blob/HEAD/crates/fmlibre-core/src/profile.rs); FM27, tool-documented, both files searched for "wage" and "demand"). And the one academic use of Football Manager data on wages that was found, a 2024 student paper on FM23's Premier League, reports that club and appearances raise the wage paid and age lowers it, with no coefficient given on the page and no ability or reputation term ([Linfield University symposium, 2024](https://digitalcommons.linfield.edu/symposium/2024/all/4); carried over from FM23, tested on wages paid, not demands).

## What drives a demand

### What Sports Interactive has said

FM27's 27.1.0 notes, as FM Scout copied them on 17 November 2026, carry two lines on contracts: "Players no longer ask for a relegation release clause higher than their minimum fee release clause" and "Fixed a freeze when discussing a playing time promise in loan talks" ([FM Scout copy of the 27.1.0 notes](https://www.fmscout.com/a-fm27-update-27.1.0.html); FM27, official, secondary). Nothing in them concerns wages.

FM26's notes say more, and are carried over from FM26, official, secondary. Update 26.0.6 of 20 November 2025: "Improved logic behind youth player wage demands", "Improved logic behind player wage demands in the lower leagues", and players "will more appropriately decline entering contract negotiations upfront if there is a large discrepancy between the desired wage and the max wage the club can offer" ([FM Scout copy of 26.0.6](https://www.fmscout.com/a-fm26-update-26.0.6.html); [Operation Sports copy](https://www.operationsports.com/football-manager-26-update-3-finally-live-on-steam-public-beta-featuring-200-fixes-and-new-improvements-patch-notes/); the last line was cut short in both fetches and its ending is from a search summary). Update 26.1.0 of 4 December 2025: "Improved advice consistency between Transfer and Contract negotiations", "Player wage will now display correctly even if exceeding the maximum amount the user can offer", "Tweaks to player interest for high-profile free agents" and "Improved wage and transfer budget distribution logic for AI clubs" ([FM Scout copy of 26.1.0](https://www.fmscout.com/a-fm26-update-26.1.0.html)). Update 26.1.1 of 10 December 2025: "Improvements to player logic for wanting a new contract", "Improved logic/messaging behind older players asking for new contracts", and a fix for "player contract demands" not displaying correctly, which a search summary completes as "when the user asks the agent about market interest for a Free Agent" ([FM Scout copy of 26.1.1](https://www.fmscout.com/a-fm26-update-26.1.1.html)).

Three facts follow, and they are the only official ones. The game computes a desired wage per player before any talk. The club has a separate ceiling on one wage offer, which the game compares with it. And lower-league and youth demands were wrong enough in FM26 at release to be changed, so any lower-league anecdote from before 20 November 2025 describes logic that no longer exists. Operation Sports reported after a later FM26 patch that "the long-running wage-demand bug is still very much alive", with a youth player on $2.9K a year demanding $300K a year on renegotiation ([Operation Sports](https://www.operationsports.com/fm26-patch-improves-performance-but-leaves-wage-bug-and-formation-issues-unfixed); snippet only, carried over from FM26, anecdotal). Whether FM27 inherited the fixed or the faulty behaviour is untested.

The FM24 induction hints, as FM Scout reproduces them, give the one official statement on what a wage tracks: "A player's wage often is comparable with his playing time", "Agreeing on a level of Playing Time gives the player an idea of how often they are likely to feature", and the wage, the signing-on fee and the agent fee "all … affect current and future budget costs"; the manager "may adjust your budget for more flexibility when negotiating wages or signing on fees" ([FM Scout, FM24 induction hints, 10 Feb 2025](https://www.fmscout.com/a-football-manager-2024-induction-hints-and-tips.html); carried over from FM24, official, secondary). No number is attached.

### The club's side: the budget, the board's cap, and what others are paid

The demand is reported to rise with what the club can pay. A 2014 thread on SI's forum has a user who tested the contract screen and found that "the more you have available in your wage budget, the higher the contract demand will be for the player", with a reply that "clubs know how much you have to spend, and will adjust their demands accordingly" ([SI forum, topic 315271](https://community.sports-interactive.com/forums/topic/315271-player-contract-demands-vs-available-budgets); snippet only, carried over from about FM14, one user's test with no numbers seen). An FM24 bug report says "the engine is looking into your available budget and making requests based on that", and a forum poster, in a search summary that did not name the version, that "players started asking for silly wage demands as soon as I had a surplus in my wage budget" (snippets only; carried over, anecdotal). A Steam poster on FM24 adds that players "demand more when others at the same level are paid more" ([Steam FM24, Oct 2023](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2252570/discussions/0/3879347492856315761/)), and an FM23 manager whose director of football gave a youth player £275K a week found that "now all my players want increased salaries" ([FM Scout, 20 Apr 2023](https://www.fmscout.com/q-24856-Director-of-football-offered-youth-player-£275k-per-week.html)). The old "match highest earner" clause had the same effect by contract after a promotion (FM11 to FM20 forum posts, snippets only). All of that is anecdotal and carried over.

The board's cap on one wage is separate from the budget and is well attested as a nuisance. FM24 bug reports describe a manager with "over 60k per week in the wage budget available" whose board "will only let me offer roughly half" of the £3K to £4K a week two players asked ([SI bug tracker r17118](https://community.sports-interactive.com/bugtracker/football-manager-2024-early-access-bugs-tracker/transfers-scouting-contracts-and-agentsintermediaries/cant-offer-requested-wages-despite-having-massive-excess-wage-budget-on-save-loaded-from-fm23-r17118)), a maximum of £7,250 a week for a young squad player, "around a fifth of what he was asking for" ([r18497](https://community.sports-interactive.com/bugtracker/football-manager-2024-early-access-bugs-tracker/finances-training-medical-and-development-centres/max-wage-amount-allowed-by-board-is-very-low-for-young-players-r18497)), and a squad revolt because a team leader could not be renewed under it ([r18870](https://community.sports-interactive.com/bugtracker/football-manager-2024-early-access-bugs-tracker/finances-training-medical-and-development-centres/players-revolt-cant-renew-contract-with-team-leader-max-wage-limited-by-board-r18870)); all snippets only, carried over from FM24, anecdotal. FMRTE 27 shows the same thing as a number: on the club Finances page, Wage Budget is the "total wage allowance", Wage Used the "current wage spending", and **"Max Wage (aprox.)" the "approximate individual wage limit", "a separate limit for an individual offer"** ([docs.fmrte.com, Club finances](https://docs.fmrte.com/clubs/finances/); FM27, tool-documented). The page names no period for any of them and tells the reader to check FMRTE's wage setting against the game's.

### The player's side: playing time, reputation, age, form and promotion

Playing time is the one driver with official words behind it, quoted above, and guides back to FM16 repeat it: "the salary that you offer to the player is connected to" his status, and "when offering someone the key player status, don't expect that he will lower his requirements" ([Gamepressure, FM16](https://www.gamepressure.com/footballmanager2016/contract-negotiations/za8378); carried over from FM16, anecdotal). The direction is contested at the margin: one summary of SI forum topic 584210 has a user saying a lesser role pushes the wage asked up, as compensation, against a greater one (snippet only, carried over from about FM24). No test of the same player offered two playing times was found.

Reputation appears in every list of causes and in no measurement. An FM24 poster reports that players with a low reputation or a long time without a club "have unrealistic demands and demand much higher salaries", and the reply lists "Club Reputation, League Reputation, Finances, Coaches, Facilities and many more" ([FM Scout, May 2024](https://www.fmscout.com/q-25855-What-determines-whether-the-players-are-interested-in-negotiating-or-not.html)); a Steam poster says dropping to a lower level "can sometimes see players demand higher wages, to 'drop down' that far" (snippet only). Carried over from FM24, anecdotal. FM13 posters say players of 35 and over accept less, young players in form ask more, and a squad asks for rises after winning a league ([FM Scout, June 2013](https://www.fmscout.com/q-9169-Player-Contracts.html); carried over from FM13, anecdotal). Nothing was found on Ambition, Loyalty, an agent's traits or morale with any number; Gamepressure's FM22 guide says only that the agent's "patience … may run dry quickly" and that he "lowered his expectations later on" after a rejected counter ([Gamepressure, FM22](https://www.gamepressure.com/football-manager-2022/negotiating-contracts/zff1ce)).

Promotion is the largest reported effect. On FM24 a manager in the National League saw players wanting about £1.5K a week, and after promotion to League Two about £15K a week, "ten times at that level??"; another, on FM23, had "literally the entire team" ask for new contracts after promotion to the Bundesliga, a captain for "four times his current wage" ([Steam FM24, 24 to 25 Oct 2023](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2252570/discussions/0/3879347492856315761/)). An FM24 poster had players asking "at least 4 times what they are making now" within six to twelve months of signing, one ten times ([FM Scout, 28 Nov 2023](https://www.fmscout.com/q-25419-Contract-renewals-make-no-senseIs-it-only-me.html)). Those are carried over from FM23 and FM24, anecdotal, two or three saves, and all predate FM26's change to lower-league demands.

### The terms: what trades against the wage

No source gives an exchange rate between a wage and a clause. The directional claims, all anecdotal and carried over: a loyalty bonus "is counted out from the budget once and it can convince the player to agree on a lower wage", a yearly wage rise compounds because "the percentage is counted from the current wage", and a larger agent fee can buy a lower wage ([Gamepressure, FM16](https://www.gamepressure.com/footballmanager2016/contract-negotiations/za8378)); an appearance fee is paid every match, so on a regular it is wage by another name, and "a player asking for a lower weekly wage may still become expensive if the contract includes large bonuses and fees" ([FIFPlay, FM27 financial tips, undated](https://www.fifplay.com/football-manager-27-tips-financial-management/); FM27, anecdotal, a general guide with no data). One FM24 bug report says that changing any single term made "every single money-based demand" rise by 38 to 40% ([SI bug tracker r22144](https://community.sports-interactive.com/bugtracker/football-manager-2024-early-access-bugs-tracker/transfers-scouting-contracts-and-agentsintermediaries/agent-demanding-~40-more-money-on-wages-bonuses-due-to-one-suggested-change-r22144); snippet only), which, bug or not, says the opening demand is not a floor that bargaining only lowers.

| Driver | What is known | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Desired wage against the club's maximum wage | The game compares them before talks | Carried over from FM26, official |
| Division | Lower-league logic changed in 26.0.6; ten times at promotion in one report | Official (that it changed); anecdotal (the size) |
| Playing time agreed | "Often comparable" with the wage | Carried over from FM24, official; no number |
| Wage budget available | More room, higher demand | Carried over from about FM14, one user's test, snippet only |
| Other players' wages | Rises spread through the squad | Carried over from FM23 and FM24, anecdotal |
| Player's and club's reputation | Named everywhere, measured nowhere | Anecdotal |
| Age, form, a title | Older accept less; in-form young ask more | Carried over from FM13, anecdotal |
| Ambition, Loyalty, agent traits, morale | Nothing with a number found | Gap |
| Clauses and bonuses | Loyalty bonus and agent fee lower the wage; no rate | Carried over from FM16, anecdotal |
| Current wage | Demands reported at 4 and 10 times it | Carried over from FM24, anecdotal |

## What the game shows, and what no tool shows

The game shows a demand at three moments, and the owner can type any of them in. First, a scout report can carry a wage estimate: on FM24 one user had "wage demands in fully scouted players that were part of my scouting focus", and another said the figures "aren't always accurate though they do give you a ballpark" ([Steam FM24, 29 Oct 2023](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2252570/discussions/0/3878221733396443573); carried over from FM24, anecdotal). The estimate can be far out: an FM23 bug report has young players "predicted to ask for something in the region of 20k p. w." asking for 110K, and SI forum topic 584210 is titled "What causes this disparity between estimated wages and actual wage demands", with a reply that the report may predate a promotion (snippets only). Second, the "Ask Agent About Availability" quick chat, "first introduced in FM22" and upgraded for FM24 ([footballmanager.com, 21 Sep 2023](https://www.footballmanager.com/node/773); official, seen as a search summary), which Sortitoutsi's FM26 buying guide describes as giving a rough expected wage, a ballpark fee and the player's interest (snippet only); FM26's 26.1.1 fixed how "player contract demands" display in the agent chat for a free agent, so the chat does show them. Third, the contract offer screen itself, where the agent states his terms or asks for an offer. FM24 reports say the first two can disagree with the third: an agent who agreed in the chat to lower the wage raised it once talks opened ([r17437](https://community.sports-interactive.com/bugtracker/football-manager-2024-early-access-bugs-tracker/transfers-scouting-contracts-and-agentsintermediaries/agent-says-player-would-accept-lower-wage-but-then-increases-demands-after-any-negotiation-r17437); snippet only). The figure worth typing is therefore the one on the contract screen, or the agent's when talks have not opened, with its date.

No tool shows it. FMRTE's Contracts page documents wages, dates, Agreed Playing Time, transfer and loan status, bonuses and clauses, and nothing on a wage asked ([docs.fmrte.com, Contracts](https://docs.fmrte.com/players/contracts/)); the field maps of FMSuperScout and FMLibreEditor hold none; Genie Scout 26's release notes list no such column (snippet only), and its author's 2011 remark above explains why. FMSuperScout's own model treats wages only as a proxy and a ceiling: it ranks wages within a selling club to guess squad status ("top-2 earner ≈ star player … bottom 40% ≈ fringe player", for clubs with at least eight known wages) and takes "wage feasibility" as a "ceiling from your own squad" ([FMSuperScout value model](https://github.com/mavarobli/FMSuperScout/blob/main/docs/value-model.md); FM27, a tool author's heuristic). That is the only precedent found for a tool reading a wage limit off the user's own squad.

## Timing: early is the only advice, and none of it is measured

Officially, "once players enter the final six months of their contracts, they are free to negotiate with new clubs", and the same applies to the manager's own players ([footballmanager.com, The Dugout, 18 Mar 2026](https://www.footballmanager.com/the-dugout/10-tips-successful-mid-season-transfer-window-fm26); carried over from FM26, official). Guides say to renew or sell with about 12 months left, or to sell at 18 ([FootballGPT, FM26, undated](https://footballgpt.co/fm/fm-finances); anecdotal, a commercial page with no data), and FIFPlay's FM27 page says not to "wait until the final months". **No source says whether the demand itself rises as the contract runs down**, in the last 12 months or the last 6, and none compares a renewal before a promotion with one after it; the promotion anecdotes above say only that waiting until after is expensive. For a free agent the demand is reported to fall with time unattached, though an FM24 bug report says the lower figure showed "only … in their initial contract proposal and reset after negotiating" ([r21065](https://community.sports-interactive.com/bugtracker/football-manager-2024-early-access-bugs-tracker/transfers-scouting-contracts-and-agentsintermediaries/changed-contract-demands-only-applying-to-initial-contract-proposal-r21065); snippet only), and another has one agent quoting £12.75K and £15.5K a week a month apart.

Nothing was found on how long an agent refuses to talk after negotiations break down, or on whether each renewal raises the next demand. What exists is the FM24 complaint that players "keep asking for a new contract every 6 months" (snippet only), FM26's two lines on players wanting new contracts, and an FM19 poster's advice that a player who is only "starting to feel" he deserves a new contract can be left alone (snippet only). Whether unhappiness or a poor run lowers a demand is not attested either way. The earlier research's point that a player under 24 brings compensation when he leaves at the end of his contract is in `docs/Research/fm27-sale-and-loan-offer-acceptance.md` and is not repeated.

## Division levels: the wage budget roughly doubles with each promotion

`docs/Research/fm27-sale-and-loan-offer-acceptance.md` holds FM Scout's FM27 day-one budgets by division ([FM Scout, 1 Nov 2026](https://www.fmscout.com/a-fm27-starting-transfer-and-wage-budgets.html)); they are cited here, not redone. One row was read again for this report: **Oxford City, in National League North, have a wage budget of £16K a week with £0 of it free** on day one, above the division's median of £12.5K. The medians, in £K a week, run 12.5 (National League North), 14 (South), 30 (National League), 63.5 (League Two), 135 (League One) and 388 (Championship): a factor of 2.1 to 2.4 at each step to League One and 2.9 to the Championship. Within a division the spread is as large as a step: 5.7 to 26 in National League North, 20 to 63 in the National League. A range by division alone would therefore be wrong for most clubs by a factor of two; it has to come from the club's own budget.

The table turns a budget into per-player figures by the one rule found (next section). The division rows are this report's arithmetic on FM Scout's medians, FM27, derived; "mean of 25" assumes 25 paid players, which is a guess, and whether the budget also carries youth and non-playing wages was not established.

| Wage budget, a week | Mean of 25 | Key player (7.5%) | Regular starter (4.3%) | Backup (2.7%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| National League North median, £12.5K | £500 | £940 | £540 | £340 |
| National League South median, £14K | £560 | £1,050 | £600 | £380 |
| Oxford City, £16K | £640 | £1,200 | £690 | £440 |
| National League median, £30K | £1,200 | £2,250 | £1,290 | £820 |
| League Two median, £63.5K | £2,540 | £4,760 | £2,720 | £1,730 |

Set against it, the Steam report of £1.5K a week asked in the National League sits between a starter's and a key player's ceiling at the median club, and the £15K a week asked after promotion to League Two is three times the key-player ceiling there. By this rule the first was ordinary and the second unreasonable, which is how the poster read them.

## Lower-league contract types

What a club may offer follows its status, and the evidence is thin and old. Officially, a contract offer says "whether you're offering a full-time or a part-time contract", and "most of the time this will be determined by the club's professional status" (FM24 induction hints, above; the second sentence from a search summary of an FM22 guide). On FM24, a club that had just turned semi-professional could offer players only "no wage, just appearance bonus and some other bonuses", which a reply called normal non-contract terms ([FM Scout, 6 to 8 Jan 2024](https://www.fmscout.com/q-25550-Amateur-to-Semi-Pro.html)), and an FM24 bug report is titled "Can only offer non-contract despite having wage budget" (title only). On FM13, an eighth-tier board turned its club part-time, then "allowed me to offer them only part time contracts, no amateur or non contracts", capped at £25 a week where players wanted at least £130, and the manager lost 90% of his players; another board allowed three years at most until the club turned professional ([FM Scout, Jan 2013](https://www.fmscout.com/q-7680-Contract-negotinations-English-8th-tier.html)). Old guides say a non-contract player is paid per appearance and "may move freely from the club without requiring the club's approval", that an amateur is paid nothing, and that a youth contract is "minimum wage but must be replaced later" (snippets only; FM19 to FM22). All of that is anecdotal and carried over. Nothing was found on whether a part-time club may offer one player full-time terms in FM27, or on what a youth-contract player asks when he turns professional, beyond FM26's unexplained line on youth demands and one unanswered FM26 post that youth players "refuse to sign a Youth Contract" and show "very high contract demands" ([FM Scout, 28 Jan 2026](https://www.fmscout.com/q-34372-Question-Regarding-Youth-Contract-Issues-in-FM26.html)).

The repository's captures add what the owner's own save shows. In his first capture every first-team player is full-time on £500 to £800 a week and the under-21s on £435 to £500; in the later one three players of 16 are on youth contracts at $291 a month each; and of 109 players his search returned at other clubs, 41 were part-time, 41 amateur, 8 full-time, 4 out of contract and 15 without a club. At his level most of the market is part-time or unpaid, and his own club is not.

## What the owner's own captures show

This section is this report's own measurement, made on 25 November 2026 on three files under `LineupEngine/Tests/LineupEngineTests/Fixtures/captures/`. It measures wages paid, not wages asked, on small samples from one save, and it is the only place in this report where numbers were compared.

| Capture | Players with a wage | Ability | Wage | Rank correlation of wage with ability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `first-team` (pounds a week) | 17, all full-time | 56 to 73 | £500 to £800; median £600 | 0.59 |
| `first-team-with-expiry` (23 Nov 2026, dollars a month) | 27 full-time | 67 to 109 | $1,912 to $8,724; quartiles $3,269, $3,905, $4,989 | 0.11 |
| `scouting-search` (19 Nov 2026, dollars a month; other clubs) | 49 | 60 to 75 | $291 to $5,536; quartiles $1,099, $1,620, $2,972 | 0.00 |

In the later squad the wage has a rank correlation of 0.06 with world reputation and 0.31 with age, while reputation and ability correlate at 0.76 with each other; the medians by agreed playing time do not fall in order (four Regular Starters at $3,054 a month, five Fringe Players at $4,324, six Important Players at $3,596). The top wage is 2.2 times the median and 7.7% of the full-time wage bill (that player carries a loan contract; the next wage is 1.9 times the median); in the first capture the top earner was at 1.33 times the median and 5% of the £16K budget, and the 22 paid players in the first-team and under-21 captures together drew about £12,900 a week. In the search, players of the same ability band are paid from $291 to $5,536 a month by club: the top figures are at Bolton Wanderers, Worthing, Hednesford Town and Barrow. Several wages repeat exactly ($4,079 three times in the search; $3,269 five times and $2,786 three times in the squad), which suggests the game sets wages in steps or bands.

Three readings follow, each speculation beyond the numbers. Within the range a club at this level actually holds, ability explains little of what a player is paid and the club explains most, so "what my players of similar ability earn" is a poor predictor and "what my club pays a player in his role" a better one. The weak tie in the later squad is partly a narrow range and partly history: players signed in a lower division keep their wage until renewed, so a promoted squad's wages lag what the same players would ask now. And the top earner's share, 5 to 8% of the bill, sits at or under the 7.5% the rule of thumb allows a key player.

## Rules of thumb on what is too much

Every rule here is an author's habit, labelled anecdotal even where the site is official, and none is a game mechanic.

The fullest is from an article on Sports Interactive's own site for FM23: keep four Important players, seven Regular Starters and eleven backups, split the weekly wage budget 30%, 30%, 30% and 10% across those three tiers and the youth team, and divide each share by its head count for a per-player ceiling; "any more than the above in each area means you have a bloated squad and are overpaying in wages", and a player who earns more than his tier's ceiling is one the club cannot afford ([footballmanager.com, Matthew Hallsworth, "Eight ways to manage debt in FM23"](https://footballmanager.com/node/665); snippet only, carried over from FM23). FM Inside's wage cap guide uses the same 30/30/30/10 split with at most four key players ([FM Inside](https://fminside.net/guides/financial-guides/54-how-to-use-a-wage-cap-in-football-manager); snippet only). Worked through, that is 7.5%, 4.3% and 2.7% of the budget a head.

FootballGPT's FM26 page puts it relative to the top earner: "your highest earner sets the ceiling", squad players at 40 to 60% of him and youth players at 10 to 20%, with a lower-league club's wages under 60% of its income ([FootballGPT](https://footballgpt.co/fm/fm-finances); a commercial page, no data). An FM Scout guide of 2018 keeps three to five key players "almost irreplaceable" on the top band and removes bonuses where it can ([FM Scout, 28 Sep 2018](https://www.fmscout.com/a-top-tips-for-healthy-finances-on-football-manager.html)). FIFPlay's FM27 page says to "avoid offering first-team salaries to players who will spend most matches on the bench", and that "there is no fixed percentage that works for every team". One FM24 poster would have accepted a rise of "some 50%" at a renewal and thought four times absurd. No rule relating a wage to a player's transfer value was found, other than the loan rule of value ÷ 192 a week in the earlier research, which was written for a borrower's tolerance, not a salary. Percentages attributed to FootballGPT in search summaries (a greater role lowering a demand by 10 to 20%, a month unattached by 15 to 30%) were not on the pages when read, and are not used.

## Conclusion

The search for a wage formula ends where the search for an acceptance rule did: the game's calculation is unpublished, and what has been written about it is direction without size. The directions point away from the player. What a player asks is reported to follow the club's budget, the role offered and the going rate around him, and the owner's own data agree as far as wages paid can: at one level of ability his search found wages nineteen times apart, sorted by club. A tool that priced a player from his ability or reputation would be inventing a relationship nobody has shown.

What the tool can say honestly is narrower and still useful. It can say that a quoted wage is over the club's limit, that it is above what any player in the same tier of this squad is paid, that it exceeds the share of the budget one rule of thumb gives that tier, and how many times his present wage it is, each with its source and "this tool's estimate" on everything but the limit. It should ask for the agent's figure and not guess it. And it should keep the record: the opening demand, the terms agreed and the club's three finance figures for each negotiation are numbers no forum holds, and a season of them would let the shares above be replaced by ones measured in the owner's own save.
